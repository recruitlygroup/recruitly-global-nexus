## The Problem: Vacancies on the Ward Have Real Consequences

### What nursing gaps cost a hospital

- **Patient safety and care quality:** Understaffed wards stretch teams and increase risk.
- **Staff burnout and turnover:** Overtime and covering shifts drive existing nurses to leave, deepening the gap.
- **Bed and service closures:** Capacity is reduced when rosters cannot be filled.
- **Agency and overtime costs:** Short-term cover is expensive and unstable.

### Why domestic supply is not enough

Germany has one of Europe's oldest populations, which keeps increasing demand for hospital and long-term care. Federal authorities treat nursing as a shortage occupation, and recent policy changes have aimed to speed up the entry of qualified foreign nurses.

### Why many international hires stall

Employers often find that candidates marketed as "ready" are not. Common stumbling blocks include:

- **Language levels below B2**, which block full professional licensing
- **Incomplete or inconsistent credential documents**, which slow recognition
- **Weak coordination between candidate, employer, recognition authority and embassy**
- **Unclear timelines**, which make rostering and budgeting difficult

## What Germany Requires of Foreign-Trained Nurses

Nursing is a **regulated profession** in Germany. For nurses trained outside the EU, EEA and Switzerland, the core requirements are as follows.

### 1. Recognition of the qualification (Anerkennung)

The competent state authority compares the foreign qualification with the German **Pflegefachperson** qualification. Outcomes can include full recognition or a decision that compensation measures are needed, such as a knowledge test (Kenntnisprüfung) or an adaptation course. The authority responsible depends on the federal state where the nurse will work.

### 2. German language at B2

B2 German is the standard requirement for the nursing licence. Candidates should hold a certificate from a recognized exam provider, and some federal states also require a **specialist medical-language test (Fachsprachprüfung)**. Requirements vary by state, so employers should check with the authority in their Land.

### 3. Supporting documents

Typical requirements include certified copies and translations of qualification and training records, proof of professional experience, a **medical fitness certificate**, and a **police clearance certificate**.

### 4. Employment offer and visa

A job offer and the appropriate residence permit are needed. Routes include skilled-worker pathways under Germany's Skilled Immigration Act and, for qualifying roles, the EU Blue Card, with a shortage-occupation salary threshold reported as €45,934.20 gross per year in 2026. Where recognition is incomplete, there are routes to enter Germany to complete compensation measures.

### Recent policy developments

Reports indicate that Germany introduced a **fast-track nursing visa in March 2026**, with targets of about eight weeks for consulate visa processing and about four weeks for recognition decisions. Target times are not guarantees, and actual timelines vary by authority and case. **Always confirm current rules and timelines on the official portals**, such as *Anerkennung in Deutschland* and *Make it in Germany*.
