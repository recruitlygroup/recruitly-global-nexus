**Italy offers one of the most affordable routes in Europe to a nursing degree, but it is also one of the most bureaucratic.** The tuition at public universities can be very low, a small number of programmes are taught entirely in English, and the country has a well-documented shortage of nurses. At the same time, seats for international applicants are limited, the admission calendar is strict, and working as a nurse in Italy eventually requires Italian.

This guide gives you the full picture in one place: how the degree works, which programmes teach in English, how admission tests and the visa process run, what it really costs, how scholarships work, and what a nursing career in Italy looks like afterwards. It is the first post in our **Study Nursing Abroad: Italy** series, and each section links to a deeper guide.

> **Last verified: 10 November 2026.** Admission rules, seats, fees and deadlines change every year. Where we quote 2026/27 figures, treat them as a planning template for the 2027/28 intake, and always confirm against the university's official call for applications (*bando*) before you pay or apply.

## Why Study Nursing in Italy?

### 1. A qualification built for the European market

The Italian nursing degree is a first-cycle university degree that **qualifies you to practise**: the final exam carries professional value, so there is no separate national licensing exam after graduation. Training follows the EU's minimum standard for general-care nurses, which makes the degree portable across Europe and an accepted starting point for registration elsewhere (see [working abroad](#working-abroad-with-an-italian-nursing-degree)).

### 2. Low tuition at public universities

Public universities charge according to family income, and the lowest bracket is only the regional tax plus a stamp duty. In Lazio, for example, that equals €156 (€140 regional tax plus a €16 stamp). Compare that with nursing programmes in the UK, Australia or the US, and Italy is dramatically cheaper. Your real costs are housing, food and living expenses, which we cover in our cost guide.

### 3. Hospital training from early on

Italian nursing programmes combine lectures with supervised clinical placements in teaching hospitals and local health trusts. You are not just studying theory for three years and then hoping for placements.

### 4. A health system that needs nurses

Italy has a widely reported shortage. A 2026 report covered by Italian media put the gap at roughly 65,000 nurses, and another analysis counted about 5,770 Italian nurses leaving the country in 2025, mainly for the UK and Switzerland. The shortage is real, but it comes with an important caveat: **it is partly driven by pay and working conditions**, which we address honestly in the salary section.

### 5. Life in Italy

Rome, Milan, Naples, Palermo and the smaller university towns each offer a very different cost of living and student experience. Choosing a city is as much a financial decision as an academic one.

## How the Italian Nursing Degree Works

### The BSc: *Laurea in Infermieristica*

- **Duration:** 3 years (180 credits)
- **Structure:** classroom teaching (anatomy, physiology, pathology, nursing sciences, ethics), skills laboratories and supervised clinical internships
- **Outcome:** a degree with professional qualifying value, after which you register with the nursing order (OPI) to practise
- **Access:** *numero programmato* (limited places). You must pass a selection test and place within the available seats

### The MSc: *Laurea Magistrale*

After the BSc you can continue to a 2-year Master's in Nursing and Midwifery Sciences, which focuses on management, education, research and advanced practice. English-taught nursing Master's programmes are far less common than English BSc programmes, so check the course language carefully if Italian is not yet your strength.

### Is online study possible?

No. Theory elements can use digital tools, but **a legitimate nursing degree in Italy requires in-person clinical placements** in hospitals and community services. Be cautious of any agency or website claiming you can complete a nursing degree in Italy fully online.

## Can You Study Nursing in Italy in English?

Yes, but the choice is small. The vast majority of nursing programmes in Italy are taught in Italian. For example, the University of Bologna's nursing degree at its Rimini campus and the University of Pisa's nursing degree are both listed as Italian-taught. English-language BSc tracks exist at a handful of institutions.

### English-taught BSc Nursing programmes we verified for 2026/27

| University | City | Type | Language | Key admission facts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sapienza University of Rome** (Sant'Andrea programme) | Rome | Public | English | Programme started in 2016; admission exam taken in English; no English or Italian language certificate required for admission, but students are expected to learn Italian progressively for clinical training |
| **UniCamillus (Saint Camillus International University of Health Sciences)** | Rome | Private | English (classroom teaching) | Own online, proctored admission test in English; registration fee €110 in 2026; separate calls for EU and non-EU applicants |
| **University of Palermo** | Palermo | Public | English (code 2233) | National health-professions test; 26 places for non-EU candidates residing abroad and 36 for EU and non-EU residents in Italy in the 2026/27 call (subject to ministerial confirmation); B2 English required |

**Important:** other universities may offer English tracks in some years. Do not rely on third-party lists, including ours: search the course on the official **Universitaly** portal and read the current *bando*.

### Do you need Italian even in an English programme?

You do not need an Italian certificate to apply to the programmes above, but **you will need Italian to do well**. Clinical placements involve patients and colleagues who speak Italian, and professional registration afterwards involves an Italian language assessment. Start learning Italian before you arrive.

## Admission Requirements for International Students

Requirements vary slightly between universities and years, but the core pattern is consistent.

### Academic requirements

- **At least 12 years of schooling**, with a final secondary diploma that gives access to university in your home country. If your system has 10 or 11 years, UniCamillus' 2026/27 call states that the qualification can be valid if supplemented by one or two years of university study with all exams passed. Other universities may handle this differently.
- **Science background.** The tests are built around biology, chemistry, physics and mathematics, plus logic. A school science profile helps, even where it is not formally mandatory.
- **Recognition of your qualification.** Universities accept either a **CIMEA** verification/comparability certificate or a **Declaration of Value (DoV)** from the Italian embassy or consulate in the country where you studied, with a legalised translation, according to UniCamillus' call. Check which your chosen university accepts.

### Language requirements

- **English-taught programmes:** Palermo requires B2 English. Sapienza's English nursing programme states that no English certificate is needed for admission because the exam is conducted in English. UniCamillus tests in English.
- **Italian-taught programmes:** non-EU applicants abroad usually have to prove Italian, for instance through a certificate or a university-recognised test. Some universities run a CISIA-administered Italian language test for non-EU candidates.

### Documents you should expect to prepare

1. Valid passport
2. Secondary school diploma and transcripts, with legalised and translated copies
3. CIMEA statement or Declaration of Value, where required
4. Language certificate, if required by the call
5. Passport-style photograph
6. For the visa stage: pre-enrolment on Universitaly, financial proof, accommodation and health insurance, depending on your embassy
7. After enrolment: health checks and vaccinations. UniCamillus, for example, requires a tuberculin test and hepatitis B vaccination within set deadlines

## The Nursing Entrance Test

Admission to nursing in Italy is by **competitive test**, not by grades alone. How the test works depends on the university.

### Public universities: the national health-professions test

For public programmes, the Ministry of University and Research (MUR) sets the content and methods of the admission tests for health-profession degrees through a ministerial decree each year. In 2026/27 the University of Palermo's English nursing call described this format:

| Section | Questions |
|---|---|
| Reading skills and knowledge acquired in studies | 4 |
| Logical reasoning and problem solving | 5 |
| Biology | 23 |
| Chemistry | 15 |
| Physics and mathematics | 13 |
| **Total** | **60 questions, 5 options each, 100 minutes** |

**Scoring:** 1.5 points for a correct answer, −0.4 for a wrong answer and 0 for a blank, with a maximum of 90 points. Ties are broken first by biology, then chemistry, then physics and mathematics, then logic, then reading.

Notice the weighting: **biology and chemistry together make up more than 60% of the questions**. If you have limited preparation time, start there.

> **A common mix-up:** many blogs say nursing applicants must take the "TOLC-F". TOLC tests are run by CISIA and are used by other degree programmes. For the 2026/27 public nursing call we reviewed, the test followed the ministerial health-professions format instead. Always read the call for your target university and do not assume.

### Private universities: their own tests

UniCamillus' 2026/27 call for EU candidates described a **written test in English, taken remotely with online proctoring**:

- 60 multiple-choice questions: logical reasoning (20), general culture (5), biology (10), chemistry (10), physics (10), mathematics (5)
- 60 minutes
- 1 point per correct answer, −0.25 per wrong answer, 0 for blank
- Candidates who score below six out of ten in chemistry, biology or physics may be admitted with additional educational obligations (OFA), meaning extra coursework to close the gap

### How to prepare

- Build your biology and chemistry foundations first (cell biology, genetics, human physiology, stoichiometry, organic chemistry basics)
- Practise timed, negative-marked multiple-choice papers, because blind guessing is penalised
- Practise logic and reading questions, which are a small share of the public test but decide ties and show up heavily in the private test
- Rehearse the technical side if your test is remote: webcam, second camera, browser requirements and a stable connection

## Application Timeline: From Test to Visa

The Italian system moves in a fixed order, and **missing one step usually means waiting a full year**. Below is the sequence we observed in the 2026/27 cycle. Use it as a template for planning 2027/28, not as a promise of dates.

| Stage | What happens | 2026/27 example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Choose programmes** | Compare language, seats and city | Start 12 months ahead |
| **2. University pre-selection (where required)** | Some universities, such as Sapienza, run an application round for non-EU candidates abroad before Universitaly | Sapienza: 22 Dec 2025 to 15 May 2026 for non-EU candidates requiring a visa |
| **3. Register for the admission test** | Pay the test fee and upload documents | UniCamillus: by 14 July 2026 (fee €110); Palermo: payment by 24 Aug 2026 (fee €60) |
| **4. Take the test** | In person or online, depending on the university | UniCamillus: 17 July 2026, online; Palermo: 17 Sept 2026, in person |
| **5. Results and ranking** | Admitted and "eligible" lists; further candidates admitted via scrolling lists | UniCamillus results by 21 July 2026; Palermo enrolment by 25 Sept 2026 and scrolling from 29 Sept |
| **6. Pre-enrolment on Universitaly** | Mandatory for non-EU applicants living abroad; triggers the visa process | Deadlines varied by university in 2026/27, for example 31 Aug for restricted-access courses at Pavia and 30 Sept at Pisa |
| **7. Study visa (type D)** | Apply at the Italian embassy or consulate after pre-enrolment is validated | Embassy deadlines can be earlier than Universitaly's |
| **8. Arrive and enrol** | Residence permit request within eight days of arrival, tax code, health insurance | Some universities require visas issued by a set date (Pisa: 30 Nov 2026) |

### Why you must read each university's deadlines

Italy's overall rules come from a ministerial circular covering entry, stay and enrolment of international students, which applies to both 2026/27 and 2027/28. But **each university and each embassy sets its own operational deadlines**, and limited-access programmes like nursing often have earlier ones. The Italian circular also makes clear that a university accepting your pre-enrolment does not guarantee a visa: the consulate decides.

## How Much Does It Cost?

Costs fall into four groups.

### 1. Admission test fees

These are non-refundable. In 2026/27, Palermo charged €60 for its English nursing test and UniCamillus charged €110. Applying to more than one programme multiplies this cost.

### 2. Tuition and university contributions

- **Public universities:** income-based. Non-EU students outside Italy typically provide an **ISEE Parificato** (a special equivalent-income document), and students who do not provide it can be placed in the highest income bracket. The minimum bracket in Lazio is €156 per year including stamp duty.
- **Private universities:** tuition is set by the university and not scaled to your family's income in the same way. UniCamillus' EU call requires a first instalment of €1,500 plus a €16 stamp duty and the regional tax at enrolment. Non-EU tuition regulations may differ, so check them.

### 3. Living costs

The largest expense after tuition is usually housing. Rome and Milan are considerably more expensive than southern or smaller university cities. University canteens in Lazio, for example, charge between €2.50 and €8.40 per meal depending on ISEE, and meals can be free for eligible scholarship winners.

### 4. Hidden costs

Document legalisation and translation, CIMEA fees, visa and residence permit costs, health insurance, vaccinations and health checks, uniforms and clinical equipment.

> We avoid quoting a single "monthly budget" because the range between cities is too wide to be useful. Our dedicated cost guide breaks it down city by city.

## DSU Scholarships and the ISEE Parificato

**DSU** (*Diritto allo Studio Universitario*) is Italy's regional right-to-study system. Regional bodies such as DiSCo in Lazio, ER.GO in Emilia-Romagna or EDISU in Piedmont run annual calls offering a mix of cash grants, free or subsidised meals, and in many cases accommodation, to students who meet income and merit criteria.

### How it works for non-EU students

1. **Choose your university first,** since DSU is run by the regional body where the university is located. Deadlines can fall **before** the nursing admission test, so research the call early.
2. **Prepare your ISEE Parificato** (also called ISEE Universitario Parificato). Non-EU students living abroad cannot use the standard Italian ISEE, so their home-country income and asset documents are legalised, translated and processed at an authorised tax assistance centre (CAF). This takes months, so start early.
3. **Apply in the regional call.** In 2026/27, DiSCo Lazio's first application phase ran from 10 June to 22 July 2026, and calls elsewhere opened between June and July and closed between late July and 30 September.
4. **Meet merit requirements** in later years, such as earning a minimum number of credits.

### What can you expect?

Figures vary by region, family situation and whether you live far from the university. For Lazio in 2026/27, a funding database listed a yearly grant of roughly €2,290 to €6,571, plus two free meals per day. The Lazio income ceiling for 2026/27 was an ISEE of €28,339.88, and other regions set different ceilings, for example €27,000 in Tuscany and €26,306.25 in Piedmont.

### What to be careful about

- **Not every student qualifies**, and amounts differ widely. Blog posts promising "free study plus a €7,000 stipend for everyone from India or Pakistan" are oversimplified.
- **Deadlines are strict**, and a missed ISEE deadline can push you into a higher fee bracket. DiSCo Lazio's 2026/27 call, for instance, set 10 December 2026 as the final date for students not residing in Italy to file the ISEE Parificato.
- **Private universities do not use DSU in the same way**, although many offer their own scholarships.
- **Timing can be awkward for nursing applicants.** Lazio's 2026/27 application window closed on 22 July 2026, before the September nursing tests, so you may have to apply while your admission is still uncertain. Calls for 2027/28 are expected from roughly June 2027, but each region sets its own dates.
- **Eligible does not always mean funded.** In Palermo's 2026/27 first-year ranking, a union statement reported only 281 of 9,956 eligible students received the scholarship. Plan your budget so it works without DSU.

## Becoming a Registered Nurse in Italy

Graduating is not the last step. To practise, you must be registered with a **provincial nursing order (OPI)**, which belongs to the national federation FNOPI.

- **Qualifying exam:** the final degree exam has qualifying value for Italian graduates, so there is no extra national nursing board exam.
- **Registration:** you join the register of the OPI in the province where you work or live.
- **Language:** foreign-trained nurses must demonstrate Italian language ability, and non-EU nurses also sit an exam on the legal rules governing nursing in Italy. If you graduate from an Italian programme in English, **confirm directly with the OPI you intend to join** exactly what language evidence they ask for, because practice varies and sources disagree.
- **Employers:** job adverts for nurses in Italy commonly ask for registration and advanced Italian, with some asking for C1.

The practical takeaway: **English-taught study opens the door, Italian opens the career.** Our post on working as a nurse in Italy covers registration step by step.

## Nursing Salaries and Job Outlook

### What nurses earn

Nursing pay in Italy is modest by European standards, and anyone choosing Italy purely for salary should know this.

- A 2026 analysis of a newly signed public-health contract estimated a newly hired nurse's structural gross pay at about **€30,483 for 2026**, with net monthly pay of roughly **€1,830 to €1,870**.
- The public-sector health contract for 2025 to 2027 was signed in July 2026 and is reported to add up to about €240 gross per month at full effect, plus arrears of up to roughly €2,300 for nurses.
- Italian press reports citing a nursing profession report put the **average nurse salary at about €32,400, below the roughly €39,800 average across comparable European countries**, with regional differences: lower in parts of the south and higher in places such as Trentino-Alto Adige.
- Private-sector pay is generally similar to public pay, according to the salary analysis we reviewed.

We deliberately do not publish rupee, dirham or dollar conversions here, because exchange rates move, and many online conversions are inflated.

### The job outlook

- Italy's shortage of nurses is widely reported, with estimates around 65,000.
- Hiring plans, a renewed national contract and a political focus on community nursing suggest continued demand.
- The same reports describe why nurses leave: workload, shifts and pay compared with the UK and Switzerland.

**Our view:** Italy is a strong choice if you want an affordable, EU-based qualification and are willing to learn Italian. It is a weaker choice if your only goal is maximising income straight after graduation.

## Working Abroad with an Italian Nursing Degree

Many graduates use Italy as a springboard.

- **Within the EU/EEA:** nursing qualifications that meet the EU's minimum training requirements are generally recognised under the EU professional qualifications system. Eligibility and procedures depend on your nationality and the destination country, so confirm with that country's regulator.
- **United Kingdom:** graduates of an Italian nursing degree taught in English can apply to the Nursing and Midwifery Council for assessment, then demonstrate English proficiency and pass the Test of Competence before registration.
- **United States, Gulf states and elsewhere:** each regulator sets its own credential evaluation, exams and language rules. Treat Italian study as the educational foundation, not an automatic licence.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Relying on outdated blog posts.** Seats, fees, test formats and deadlines change every year. Check the current call.
2. **Assuming the TOLC-F is the nursing test.** Check the call for your specific university.
3. **Applying to only one programme.** Seats for non-EU applicants abroad are limited, and some calls reserve very few.
4. **Starting document legalisation late.** DoV, CIMEA, translations and the ISEE Parificato are the usual causes of missed deadlines.
5. **Believing "free education for everyone".** DSU depends on income, merit, region and deadlines.
6. **Ignoring Italian.** Even in English-taught programmes, placements and registration involve Italian.
7. **Paying unofficial intermediaries for guaranteed admission.** No agent can guarantee a seat. The test result decides.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is BSc nursing in Italy a university degree?

Yes. It is a three-year first-cycle university degree (*Laurea*) of 180 credits, and it is a qualifying degree for the nursing profession.

### Can I study nursing in Italy in English?

Yes, at a limited number of universities. For 2026/27 we verified English-taught BSc nursing at Sapienza University of Rome (Sant'Andrea), UniCamillus in Rome and the University of Palermo. Most other programmes are in Italian.

### Can I study nursing in Italy for free?

Public tuition can be very low and, with a DSU scholarship, you may receive a fee exemption, meals and sometimes housing. It is not automatic and depends on income, merit, region and meeting deadlines. You will still pay the regional tax and cover living costs unless the scholarship covers them.

### What is the entrance exam for nursing in Italy?

Public universities use a test whose format is set by the Ministry each year. In 2026/27, Palermo's English nursing test had 60 questions over 100 minutes covering biology, chemistry, physics and mathematics, logic and reading. Private universities such as UniCamillus run their own tests.

### Do I need to speak Italian?

Not necessarily to apply to an English-taught programme, but you will need Italian for clinical placements and, in most cases, for professional registration and employment.

### Is there an age limit?

The calls we reviewed did not state a maximum age, but each university's call and the Ministry's student-entry circular are the authority. Check the specific rules for your intake.

### Can I work as a nurse in the UK or USA with an Italian degree?

You may be able to, but only after meeting that country's registration process. The degree gives you the educational base. It does not replace UK NMC registration or US licensing steps.

### Can I do a Master's in nursing in Italy in English?

Options are limited. Check the University course catalogues and Universitaly for the current year.

### When should I start preparing?

Ideally 12 months before the intake. Language, documents, ISEE paperwork and test preparation all take longer than most students expect.

### Are agencies necessary?

No. You can apply directly through Universitaly and the universities' portals. A reputable adviser can save time with documents and deadlines, but they cannot guarantee admission or a scholarship.
