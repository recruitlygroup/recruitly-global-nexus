## Personal Safety & Everyday Precautions in Georgia

Major student cities such as **Tbilisi** and **Batumi** feature active public life, established international student communities, and generally low violent crime rates. Even so, smart habits protect you from common everyday risks:

* **Watch for Petty Theft & Crowds:** Keep phones, wallets, and bags secure in busy areas or on public transport, and steer clear of street demonstrations or political rallies.

* **Transport & Road Safety:** Use registered taxis or reputable ride-hailing apps rather than unmarked vehicles, and take extra care on mountain roads, uneven rural routes, and night journeys.

* **Respect Restricted Zones:** Never travel toward conflict-affected or breakaway/restricted regions (such as Abkhazia or South Ossetia) or cross unauthorized border areas.

* **Carry Key IDs & Copies:** Keep your residence card / ID details handy and store passport originals safely in your accommodation.

## Healthcare, Insurance & Emergency Readiness

Good health planning is especially important if you are studying away from home for several years:

* **Emergency Number 112:** Dial **112** in Georgia for unified police, ambulance/medical, and fire response.

* **Health & Accident Insurance:** Georgia enforces mandatory health and accident insurance requirements for foreign visitors entering the country—verify coverage limits, policy duration, and claim rules so your insurance works both at the border and if you need hospital or outpatient care.

* **Hospitals & Clinics:** Major cities like Tbilisi and Batumi have modern private clinics and hospitals, and many medical staff speak English. Always keep receipts, prescriptions, and claim records when visiting a clinic.

* **Routine Wellness:** Maintain basic prescriptions, sleep, nutrition, and stress habits—especially during intense exam periods or clinical rotations.

## Safe Housing & Living Choices

Your living arrangement affects both daily security and monthly costs:

* **Dormitories / Managed Student Housing:** Often offer structured entry controls, common facilities, and clearer rules for newcomers.

* **Shared or Private Apartments:** Popular for independence and cooking your own meals, though you should inspect locks, heating, utility meters, and lease terms carefully before signing.

* **All-Inclusive Living Option:** Students who prefer bundled meals, housing, cleaning, internet, and utilities can compare independent budgeting against an all-inclusive **\$9,000 USD/year** living package covering **3 meals a day, accommodation (with cleaning twice a week), Wi-Fi, and utility bills**.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Is Georgia safe for international students?

Georgia is generally considered safe with low violent crime rates in major university cities like Tbilisi and Batumi, provided students follow standard city precautions, avoid protests or restricted areas, and stay careful on roads.

### What emergency number is used in Georgia?

Georgia's unified emergency number is **112** for medical, police, and fire emergencies.

### Do international students need health insurance in Georgia?

Yes—foreign visitors entering Georgia are subject to valid health and accident insurance rules, and students should keep continuous coverage for their stay and check policy terms for medical claims.

### Should I use a ride app or public transit at night?

Public transit is widely used in cities like Tbilisi, while using reputable ride-hailing apps or registered taxis is recommended for late-night travel directly to your residence.
