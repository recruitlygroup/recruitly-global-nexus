## Can an MBBS Doctor Work in Germany?

Yes. Germany relies heavily on internationally trained physicians. At the end of 2025, about 71,480 doctors working in Germany did not hold German citizenship, roughly 16% of all practising doctors. Hospitals, especially outside the big cities, actively recruit foreign-trained MBBS graduates.

The route is structured and rule-based. The three things that decide your timeline are your German language level, your licensing route, and how quickly your documents are legalised and translated.

## The Big Requirement: German B2 (and Medical German C1)

German language is the single biggest hurdle. The requirements are:

- **General German at B2.** This is the entry ticket. Most authorities accept certificates such as telc B2 or equivalent recognised exams.
- **Medical German at C1, proven through the Fachsprachprüfung (FSP).** This exam is run by the regional medical chamber (Landesärztekammer). It tests patient history-taking, doctor-to-doctor handovers and clinical documentation.

A general B2 certificate alone is not enough for full licensing. A telc B2 Medizin certificate also does not replace the FSP. Many doctors lose time by studying toward C1 before they apply, when B2 is what lets you sit the FSP.

## Licensing: Approbation vs Berufserlaubnis

To practise medicine in Germany you need a licence.

- **Approbation** is the full, unrestricted licence with no time limit. It lets you practise anywhere in Germany, including independently and in senior roles.
- **Berufserlaubnis** is a temporary licence, valid for up to about two years, usually tied to one hospital and a supervising physician. It lets you start working while your recognition process continues.

### What about the Kenntnisprüfung?

If your MBBS was earned outside the EU/EEA/Switzerland, the authority compares your training with the German degree. Where there are substantial differences, you sit the **Kenntnisprüfung**, a medical knowledge exam covering areas such as internal medicine, diagnostics and pharmacology. Doctors with EU/EEA degrees generally only need the FSP and verified documents.

Typical processing time is longer for non-EU doctors. Public guides suggest roughly 6 to 18 months depending on the document assessment and whether the knowledge exam is required. Rules and processing times differ between federal states, so always confirm with the competent authority.

## Step-by-Step Pathway for MBBS Doctors

1. Start German classes and reach B2.
2. Collect and legalise your documents (degree, transcripts, registration, certificates of good standing, identity documents).
3. Get certified translations by sworn translators.
4. Choose a federal state and apply to its licensing authority.
5. Pass the Fachsprachprüfung (medical German, C1).
6. Secure a hospital job offer and the appropriate visa.
7. Start work under a Berufserlaubnis where applicable.
8. Complete the Kenntnisprüfung if required.
9. Receive your Approbation.
10. After Approbation, you can explore options such as the EU Blue Card.

## Documents Checklist

- Medical degree certificate and transcripts
- Medical council registration and work experience letters
- Certificate of good standing and police clearance
- Proof of health fitness
- Passport, CV and passport photos
- German language certificates (B2 and FSP result)
- Certified translations and legalisation or apostille as required by the authority

Authorities publish their own lists, and requirements vary by state. Treat this as a starting checklist, not a final one.

## Average Doctor Salary in Germany (2026)

Most hospital doctors are paid under collective agreements (TV-Ärzte), so pay rises with experience and duties.

- **Assistenzarzt (resident) starting pay:** roughly €5,100 to €5,800 gross per month depending on the employer. Public data shows municipal hospitals (TV-Ärzte VKA) at around €5,085 to €5,722, university hospitals (TV-Ärzte TdL) at about €5,627, and some private operators such as Helios starting near €5,842.
- **Overall average across all doctors:** about €7,600 gross per month, according to one 2025 salary overview. Another study cited by a German doctors' job portal puts the average at around €86,000 per year.
- **On-call duties and allowances** add to base pay. Residents commonly do around five to nine on-call shifts per month.
- **Regional differences:** doctors earn more in some states, such as Baden-Württemberg and Hessen, than in states such as Mecklenburg-Vorpommern.
- **Specialists (Facharzt), senior physicians and department heads** earn considerably more, and self-employed doctors with a practice can earn more again.

These are gross figures before tax and social contributions. Your actual salary depends on your contract, employer and specialty.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is B2 German mandatory for doctors in Germany?**
Yes. B2 general German is required, and you must also pass the medical language exam (FSP) at C1 level to obtain your licence.

**Do non-EU doctors need the Kenntnisprüfung?**
Often yes, when the authority finds substantial differences between your training and the German curriculum.

**Can I work before I get the Approbation?**
Possibly, on a temporary Berufserlaubnis under supervision, depending on your state and situation.

**How much does a doctor earn in Germany?**
Residents start at roughly €5,100 to €5,800 gross per month, and the overall average is higher. Pay grows with experience and specialisation.
