# Nursing Entrance Exam in Italy: Format, Scoring, Syllabus and How to Prepare (2027 Intake)

**You cannot apply to nursing in Italy on grades alone.** Nursing is a limited-access degree, so your place depends on how you score in an admission test and where that score ranks among everyone competing for the available seats. For international applicants the seats can be very few, which makes the test the single most important step in your application.

This guide explains the test that was used for English-taught nursing programmes in the 2026/27 cycle, how it is scored, what it covers, and how to prepare. It is part of our **Study Nursing Abroad: Italy** series. For the full picture of the degree, admission and visa process, start with the [complete guide to studying nursing in Italy](/blog/study-nursing-in-italy).

> **Last verified: 10 November 2026.** The format below comes from the official 2026/27 calls of Sapienza University of Rome and the University of Palermo, plus UniCamillus' call for EU candidates. The Ministry of University and Research (MUR) can change the test through its annual decree, so read your target university's current call before you rely on any number here.

## How Admission Works: Why the Test Decides Everything

Nursing degrees in Italy (class L/SNT1) are **restricted-access** programmes. Every year the Ministry allocates a number of places to each university, and those places are split into quotas. For international applicants the two quotas that matter are:

- **EU citizens and non-EU citizens legally residing in Italy**, and
- **non-EU citizens living abroad who need a study visa**.

Each quota has its own ranking list. You are ranked only against candidates in your quota, using your test score. If you place inside the number of seats, you can enrol. If you do not, you cannot start that year, regardless of your school marks.

That is why a strong test score matters more than any other part of your file, and why guides that describe nursing admission as a simple "apply and enrol" process are misleading.

## The 2026/27 Public University Test: Format at a Glance

For public universities, the Ministry sets the format and content of the health-professions admission tests through an annual decree. For 2026/27, the English-language nursing calls we reviewed at **Sapienza** and **Palermo** describe the same structure.

| Feature | 2026/27 format |
|---|---|
| **Number of questions** | 60 |
| **Answer options** | 5 per question, only one correct |
| **Time** | 100 minutes |
| **Maximum score** | 90 points |
| **Date (English-taught programmes)** | 17 September 2026 |
| **Where** | In person at the university, per the Sapienza and Palermo calls |
| **Test fee** | Sapienza €35; Palermo €60 (non-refundable) |

A test-preparation provider reported that the number of questions, duration and scoring were unchanged from 2025. Treat this as a useful signal that the format is stable, not a guarantee for 2027.

### Question distribution

| Subject area | Questions | Share of test |
|---|---|---|
| Biology | 23 | 38% |
| Chemistry | 15 | 25% |
| Physics and mathematics | 13 | 22% |
| Logical reasoning and problems | 5 | 8% |
| Reading skills and knowledge acquired in studies | 4 | 7% |
| **Total** | **60** | **100%** |

> **A note on test language.** Sapienza's English-programme call states that the questions are formulated in English. Palermo's call, although it is for an English-taught degree, says in its syllabus annex that questions are formulated in Italian. This looks like text carried over from the Italian-language call, but you should **ask the university directly** and confirm the language of the paper before you register.

## How the Scoring Works (and What It Means for Guessing)

Both calls use the same marking scheme:

- **+1.5 points** for a correct answer
- **0 points** for a blank answer
- **−0.4 points** for a wrong answer

Candidates are only ranked if their total is above zero. Sapienza states explicitly that the test is failed if you score zero or less.

### The guessing maths

With five options, a pure random guess has a 20% chance of being right:

- Expected value of a blind guess: (0.2 × 1.5) − (0.8 × 0.4) = 0.30 − 0.32 = **−0.02 points**

That is roughly break-even, with a slight loss. If you can **eliminate one option**, you are guessing among four:

- Expected value: (0.25 × 1.5) − (0.75 × 0.4) = 0.375 − 0.30 = **+0.075 points**

If you can eliminate two, the expected value rises to about +0.23 points per question.

**The practical rule:** never answer a question you cannot narrow down at all, but do answer when you can confidently rule out at least one option. The scoring is deliberately designed so that blind guessing does not pay, but informed guessing does. (This is our analysis of the published scoring, not an official recommendation.)

### Tie-breaks

If two candidates have the same score, the call ranks them by their results in this order: **biology, then chemistry, then physics and mathematics, then logical reasoning, then reading**. Biology therefore counts twice: it is the largest block of questions and the first tie-breaker.

## Where the Points Are: Subject Weights

**Biology and chemistry together make up 38 of the 60 questions, or about 63% of the test.** Physics and mathematics add another 13. Logic and reading together are only 9 questions.

This should shape how you divide your time:

| Priority | Subject | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Biology** | Largest section and first tie-breaker |
| 2 | **Chemistry** | Second largest section and second tie-breaker |
| 3 | **Physics and mathematics** | 13 questions, and often the weakest area for students with a biology-focused school profile |
| 4 | **Logic** | Only 5 questions, but they are fast marks once you know the patterns |
| 5 | **Reading and general knowledge** | 4 questions; low weight, but easy to lose through carelessness |

## The Syllabus, Section by Section

The official syllabus is published in the annex of each call. The summary below follows the annexes in the Sapienza and Palermo calls. Always download the annex for your target university and use it as your checklist.

### Biology (23 questions)

The syllabus covers:

- **Chemistry of living things:** organic molecules, enzymes, weak interactions
- **The cell:** prokaryotes and eukaryotes, viruses, membrane structure and transport, cell organelles
- **Cell cycle and division:** mitosis, meiosis, chromosomes
- **Genetics:** Mendel's laws, chromosome theory of inheritance, DNA structure and replication, the genetic code, protein synthesis, gene regulation, human genetics and hereditary diseases
- **Evolution and mutation:** natural selection, genetic basis of evolution
- **Biotechnology:** recombinant DNA and its applications
- **Anatomy and physiology of animals and humans:** tissues, organ systems, their interactions, **homeostasis**
- **Bioenergetics:** ATP, redox reactions, photosynthesis, glycolysis, aerobic respiration, fermentation

**For nursing candidates**, human anatomy and physiology, genetics and cell biology deserve the most revision time, because they are core to both the test and your first-year courses.

### Chemistry (15 questions)

- **Matter and atoms:** states of matter, elements and compounds, atomic structure, isotopes, electron configuration
- **The periodic table** and periodic properties
- **Chemical bonds:** ionic, covalent, metallic, polarity, electronegativity, intermolecular forces
- **Inorganic chemistry basics:** oxides, hydroxides, acids, salts, nomenclature
- **Reactions and stoichiometry:** moles, Avogadro's number, balancing equations
- **Solutions:** solubility and concentration
- **Equilibria, kinetics and catalysis**
- **Oxidation and reduction**
- **Acids, bases and pH:** hydrolysis, buffer solutions
- **Organic chemistry basics:** hydrocarbons, isomerism, functional groups (alcohols, ethers, amines, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, esters, amides)

Stoichiometry, pH and buffer problems, and functional groups are the areas where students most often lose time, because they combine knowledge with calculation.

### Physics and mathematics (13 questions)

**Mathematics:** algebra and equations, proportions and percentages, powers, roots and logarithms, functions and graphs, trigonometry, geometry including the Cartesian plane and conic sections, probability and statistics.

**Physics:** units and conversions, vectors, kinematics, dynamics (Newton's laws, momentum, work and energy), fluid mechanics, thermodynamics, electrostatics and electrodynamics (Coulomb's law, Ohm's law, circuits, magnetic fields).

Since this is a single combined block of 13 questions, you do not need to master all of university-level physics. Focus on **unit conversions, forces and energy, fluids, circuits and gas laws**, then percentage, exponent and logarithm manipulation in mathematics.

### Logical reasoning and problems (5 questions)

These test whether you can complete an argument consistently with its premises, in verbal or symbolic form. Practise syllogisms, conditional reasoning (if-then), sequences and abstract problem solving.

### Reading skills and knowledge acquired in studies (4 questions)

Short texts, including scientific passages, fiction or news-style articles, test comprehension, vocabulary in context and inference. A small number of questions also assess general knowledge of history and geography, national and international institutions, and basic legal, economic and citizenship concepts. Reading the news in the test language every day is the best preparation.

## OFA: The Penalty for a Weak Start

Even if you are admitted, a weak result in some subjects can leave you with **OFA** (*obblighi formativi aggiuntivi*, or additional educational obligations). These are remedial requirements you must clear in your first year.

The rules differ by university:

- **Sapienza (2026/27 English nursing call):** if your overall score is below 10, you receive OFA in biology and chemistry where you did not reach the minimum number of correct answers: **11 correct in biology** and **7 correct in chemistry**. OFAs are cleared by passing a remedial test or the relevant curricular exams. If you do not clear them within the first year, you must repeat the year and cannot sit exams for later years.
- **Palermo (2026/27 call):** OFA in biology and chemistry is not assigned if you reach at least 50% of the maximum score for that area. No OFA is assigned for the other subject areas.
- **UniCamillus (2026/27 EU call):** candidates who answer fewer than six out of ten questions correctly in chemistry, biology or physics are admitted with OFA in that subject.

**Takeaway:** avoid a situation where you pass the ranking with weak science foundations. OFAs add pressure to an already demanding first year.

## UniCamillus: A Different Test

UniCamillus, a private health-sciences university in Rome, runs its **own** admission test for its English-taught BSc Nursing. In its 2026/27 call for EU and EU-equivalent applicants:

| Feature | UniCamillus 2026/27 (EU call) |
|---|---|
| **Format** | Written test in English, taken remotely with online proctoring |
| **Questions** | 60 multiple choice, 5 options each |
| **Distribution** | Logical reasoning 20, general culture 5, biology 10, chemistry 10, physics 10, mathematics 5 |
| **Time** | 60 minutes |
| **Scoring** | +1 correct, −0.25 wrong, 0 blank (maximum 60) |
| **Date** | 17 July 2026 |
| **Registration fee** | €110, non-refundable |

Compare the weighting with the public test. At UniCamillus, **logical reasoning is a third of the paper**, while biology is only 10 questions. If you are strong at logic and weaker at biology, a private university test may suit you better. If your science background is strong, the public test rewards it more.

UniCamillus publishes separate calls for EU and non-EU candidates. The details above come from the EU call, so check the non-EU call for seats, dates and fees.

## How Competitive Is It? Seats for International Applicants

Published seat numbers show how tight the funnel is. In its 2026/27 English nursing call, **Sapienza listed 18 provisional places for EU and equivalent candidates and just 1 provisional place for non-EU candidates living abroad.** The University of Palermo's English nursing call listed **26 places for non-EU candidates residing abroad** and 36 for EU and non-EU residents. UniCamillus' EU call listed 17 seats for English-taught nursing.

Two points follow from this:

1. **Seats are provisional.** Both calls say the final numbers depend on the ministerial decree. Check the course catalogue for the final figures.
2. **Unfilled seats in the non-EU-abroad quota can move.** Both Sapienza and Palermo state that vacant non-EU-abroad places are reallocated to the other quota when the ranking lists scroll.

These numbers show why applying to several universities, and treating your test as a competitive exam, is not exaggeration. Do not rely on one application.

## Test-Day Logistics and Rules

Based on the Sapienza and Palermo calls:

- **Arrival:** candidates must be at the venue by 08:00 or 09:00 (depending on the university) for a test that starts at 11:00. Latecomers can be refused entry.
- **Identity:** bring valid ID and the payment receipt for the test fee.
- **Materials:** you receive the paper and answer sheet at the venue. **No bags, books, notes, paper, phones, smartwatches, headphones or other devices** are allowed in the room, and breaking this rule can lead to exclusion.
- **Venues:** rooms are published on the course catalogue shortly before the test (Sapienza published rooms by 15 September 2026).
- **Special needs:** candidates with disabilities or specific learning difficulties can request extra time and aids by the registration deadline. Palermo's call grants up to 50% extra time for certified disabilities and up to 30% for SLD, and Sapienza's call describes a similar request process through its inclusion office. Candidates living abroad need certificates issued in their country, legalised where required, and translated.

### Candidates living abroad: confirm how you take the test

Both calls describe the English nursing test as an **in-person** test at the university in Italy. If you live outside Italy, ask the university's international office exactly how candidates abroad are expected to sit the exam and what entry arrangements apply. Do not assume there is a remote option. For UniCamillus, the EU call describes a remote proctored test; confirm the format in the non-EU call.

## When the Test Happens: The Calendar Trap

This is where many applicants slip. In the 2026/27 cycle the sequence at Sapienza looked like this:

| Step | Date |
|---|---|
| University pre-selection for non-EU applicants abroad (English programmes) | Closed on 15 May 2026 |
| Universitaly pre-enrolment for a study visa | By 30 June 2026 |
| Call for the English nursing test published | 5 August 2026 |
| Test fee payment window | 5 August to 3 September 2026 |
| Test | 17 September 2026 |
| Ranking list | 23 September 2026 |
| First-instalment payment for those who place | By 30 September 2026 |
| Further ranking intakes | October, and possibly until 11 December 2026 |

At Palermo, the call was published on 10 August 2026, test registration closed on 24 August, the test was held on 17 September, and enrolment payment was due by 25 September, with scrolling lists from 29 September.

**What this means for you:** the visa paperwork for non-EU applicants abroad starts **months before** the test date and before the final test rules are published. The university calls themselves say they can be amended after the ministerial decree. In practice, you commit to the application path in spring and sit the exam in September.

> The 2027/28 dates have not been published as of 10 November 2026. If the pattern repeats, expect pre-selection to open around December 2026 and the test in September 2027, but verify against the official calendar.

## A Practical Preparation Plan

The Ministry publishes the syllabus, but test-preparation providers reported that no official question bank exists for the 2026 test. Some universities publish a past paper after the exam, so check your target university's website. Sapienza's call, for example, gives the syllabus but no official past papers. Plan your preparation around the syllabus and timed practice.

The plan below is **our recommendation, not an official programme**. Adjust it to your starting level.

### If you have 9 to 12 months

1. **Months 1 to 3: Build the foundation.** Work through biology and chemistry using your school textbooks and the syllabus annex as a checklist. Tick off each bullet as you master it.
2. **Months 4 to 6: Fill gaps and add physics and maths.** Fix weak topics first, then cover unit conversions, mechanics, fluids, circuits, algebra and percentages.
3. **Months 7 to 9: Move to timed practice.** Do mixed question sets, with negative marking applied, and track errors by topic.
4. **Final 6 weeks: Full mock tests.** Simulate real conditions: 60 questions, 100 minutes, no phone, no notes, and the real scoring formula.

### If you have 3 to 4 months

Prioritise ruthlessly: biology (cell, genetics, human physiology), chemistry (stoichiometry, acids and bases, organic basics), then physics and maths fundamentals. Start timed sets by week 6.

### Habits that matter

- **Keep an error log** of every wrong answer: topic, why you got it wrong (knowledge, calculation or misreading), and the correct reasoning.
- **Practise in the test language.** If the paper is in English, study scientific terms in English, even if you learned them in another language at school.
- **Do weekly full-length papers** in the last two months, in a quiet room, with a timer.
- **Revise the tie-breaker subjects** (biology, then chemistry) most, because a tie can be decided there.

## Test-Taking Strategy

- **Time:** 100 minutes for 60 questions is about 1 minute 40 seconds per question. Aim to finish a first pass in about 75 minutes, which leaves 25 minutes for flagged questions.
- **Order:** answer your strongest sections first, and skip any question that needs more than about two minutes of calculation. Return to it after the first pass.
- **Guessing:** answer when you can eliminate at least one option. Leave blank when you cannot narrow the choices at all (see the scoring maths above).
- **Read carefully:** the instructions ask you to discard incorrect, arbitrary and unlikely conclusions. Many wrong options are plausible-sounding distractors.
- **Mind the answer sheet:** transcription errors on a paper answer sheet cost real points. Check numbering every 10 questions.
- **Sleep and logistics:** since you may need to arrive hours before an 11:00 start in a different country, plan travel, accommodation and a buffer day in advance.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Using the wrong test as a reference.** Many blogs say nursing applicants take the TOLC-F. In the 2026/27 public English nursing calls we reviewed, the test followed the ministerial health-professions format, and UniCamillus ran its own test. Read the call.
2. **Registering for the wrong test code.** Sapienza's call warns that registering for different tests, even by clerical error, can mean you are not allowed to take the test.
3. **Missing the fee deadline.** Payment is the registration. No payment by the deadline means no test, and fees are non-refundable.
4. **Ignoring negative marking** and guessing every question.
5. **Neglecting physics and maths** because they are only 13 questions. They can decide close rankings.
6. **Leaving language to the last minute.** Even an English-taught degree involves Italian in clinical placements.
7. **Relying on one university.** With 1 to 26 seats in the non-EU-abroad quota, a single application is a high-risk plan.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the entrance exam for nursing in Italy?

For public universities, it is a test whose format is set by the Ministry each year. In 2026/27, the English-taught nursing test had 60 multiple-choice questions with five options, 100 minutes, and covered biology, chemistry, physics and mathematics, logic and reading. Private universities such as UniCamillus run their own tests.

### Is the TOLC-F the nursing entrance test?

Not in the 2026/27 public English nursing calls we reviewed. Always check the call for your specific university and year.

### How is the Italian nursing test scored?

In the Sapienza and Palermo 2026/27 calls: +1.5 for a correct answer, −0.4 for a wrong answer and 0 for a blank, with a maximum of 90 points.

### Should I guess if I do not know the answer?

Blind guessing is roughly break-even under this scoring, so it does not help. Guessing after eliminating at least one option has a positive expected value.

### Which subjects matter most?

Biology (23 questions) and chemistry (15 questions) make up about 63% of the paper, and biology and chemistry are also the first two tie-breakers.

### Are there official past papers?

The calls we reviewed publish the syllabus but not a question bank, and a prep provider reported that no official question bank exists for 2026. Some universities publish a past paper after the test, so check your target university's website.

### What is OFA?

Additional educational obligations: remedial requirements in biology or chemistry that you must clear in your first year if your test result in those subjects is below the university's threshold.

### Can I take the nursing test online?

The 2026/27 Sapienza and Palermo English nursing tests were in person. UniCamillus' EU call described an online proctored test. Confirm the format in the call that applies to your citizenship and residence.

### How many times can I attempt the test?

You can sit the test again in the next admission cycle. Each cycle has its own calls, fees and deadlines, and as a non-EU candidate abroad you would normally repeat the pre-enrolment process.

### When is the 2027 test?

Not yet announced as of 10 November 2026. The 2026 English-taught test was on 17 September, and we will update this post when the 2027 calendar is published.
