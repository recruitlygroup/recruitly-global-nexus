# Working While Studying in Georgia (2026/27): Labour Migration Rules, Work Permits, Remote Work & Academic Balance

**TL;DR:** Holding a **Category D3 Study Visa** or a **Student Temporary Residence Permit** in Georgia authorizes your **studies**, not automatic local employment. Under Georgia's updated **Law on Labour Migration** (which took effect on **1 March 2026** and underwent further legislative/regulatory updates in mid-to-late 2026), paid local employment or self-employment generally requires obtaining a separate **right to work** rather than relying on student enrollment alone. In addition, keeping your **Temporary Residence Permit** valid depends on active university enrollment, academic progress, and staying within legal absence limits—so students should never treat side income as a replacement for funding their tuition and living expenses or compromise their grades.

## The Legal Reality in 2026/27: Study Status vs. Right to Work

Older online posts often claimed that international students in Georgia could work freely without extra steps or rely on a blanket "20-hour rule." Georgia's **Law on Labour Migration**—which took effect on **1 March 2026**—requires foreign nationals undertaking paid employment or self-employment to address **right-to-work** rules separately from their immigration/residence status unless a specific statutory exemption applies.

* **Study Residence Permit ≠ Automatic Work Permit:** A student residence permit is tied to your qualifying studies at an authorized institution, not a blanket authorization for local paid labor.

* **Employer & Position Link:** Where a work permit / right to work applies to local employment, it is typically tied to the specific employer and role under the State Employment Support Agency's electronic procedure rather than a general student card.

* **Verify Before Starting Any Job:** Never start paid local work based on informal promises from peers, social media groups, or recruiters—confirm exact statutory requirements and required filings in writing before beginning.

## What About Remote Work, Internships & Freelancing?

Students frequently ask how online work, internships, or remote roles fit into life in Georgia:

* **Remote or Cross-Border Work:** Rules around remote work, freelancing, or short-term project exemptions depend on the exact statutory wording and implementing regulations in force, as well as tax and immigration rules—verify your specific arrangement before starting.

* **Academic Placements & Internships:** Clinical rotations or curriculum-linked training differ from independent paid employment; always check with your university's international office and relevant authorities regarding what is permitted.

* **Avoid Unlawful Employment Risks:** Working without required authorization can expose both worker and employer to administrative fines and legal complications.

## Protecting Your Student Residence Permit While Studying

Your primary legal reason for being in Georgia on a student permit is your degree program:

* **Maintain Enrollment & Academic Standing:** Following the September 2026 regulatory updates, study permits are tied to active enrollment at authorized higher education institutions, and failing study requirements or losing student status can put your residence permit at risk.

* **Watch Time Outside Georgia:** Extended time spent outside Georgia beyond permitted limits without valid justification can also affect residence permit compliance—track your travel and keep supporting documentation if needed.

* **Budget Full Costs Upfront:** Plan your tuition and living costs (whether budgeting independently or via an all-inclusive **\$9,000 USD/year** living package covering 3 meals a day, accommodation with twice-weekly cleaning, Wi-Fi, and utilities) around your primary savings/sponsor rather than assuming immediate part-time earnings.

## Practical Tips for Balancing Study, Budget & Life in Georgia

* **Prioritize Exams & Clinical/Lab Schedules:** Degrees such as **MD / Medicine**, **Dentistry**, **Engineering**, and **Computer Science** carry heavy coursework and lab/clinical hours that leave limited room for long shifts.

* **Keep Official Records Organized:** Retain copies of your university enrollment certificates, residence card/decision, insurance, and any official approvals.

* **Check Updates Regularly:** Georgia's migration and labor rules have seen active changes in 2026, so always verify current requirements with official agencies or your university.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Can international students automatically work part-time in Georgia with a student residence permit?

No—holding a student residence permit or D3 visa does not automatically grant local employment rights; under Georgia's Law on Labour Migration (effective 1 March 2026), paid work or self-employment generally requires separate right-to-work authorization unless a statutory exemption applies.

### Can poor grades or dropping out affect my Georgia student residence permit?

Yes—a student residence permit depends on your valid student status at an authorized institution, so failing study requirements, suspension, or losing enrollment can end your permit eligibility.

### Should I rely on part-time income to pay my Georgia tuition and living expenses?

No—you should cover tuition and living costs through your primary study budget and proof of funds, treating any lawful work only as secondary once all legal requirements are met.
