# Bringing Your Family from Nepal to Bulgaria: A Complete Family Reunification Guide for Workers and Students

## Introduction

If you're already living in Bulgaria as a worker or student and holding a valid Bulgarian residence permit, bringing your spouse or children to join you is a real, well-established legal process — not a long shot. Bulgaria's family reunification rules, based on EU Directive 2003/86/EC, apply to both workers and students holding a valid residence permit, and the process is more accessible than in many other EU countries.

This guide walks through exactly who qualifies, what the timeline realistically looks like, and — critically — how to handle document legalization from Nepal, since Bulgaria doesn't have an embassy in Kathmandu, which adds an extra step most people don't expect. We support the entire process from start to finish, so you're not navigating this alone.

---

## Who Can Sponsor Family Reunification?

Both **workers and students** holding a valid Bulgarian residence permit can act as sponsors for family reunification, provided you meet the standard requirement:

- **You must hold your Bulgarian residence permit for at least one year** before you're eligible to apply for family reunification under the general third-country national regime. This applies whether your permit is based on employment or study.
- **Exception:** If you hold an **EU Blue Card** (for highly qualified employment), you can sponsor family reunification immediately, without the one-year wait. This is a narrower category and won't apply to most workers, but it's worth checking if your role qualifies.

---

## Who Can You Bring?

Under the standard third-country national process, you can bring:

- **Your spouse** (marriage or, in some cases, a documented long-term cohabiting partnership)
- **Your minor, unmarried children**, including your spouse's children from a previous relationship

**Dependent parents** are only eligible under a narrower provision that generally requires the sponsor to hold **permanent residence** (not just a standard temporary permit) and to demonstrate the parents are financially dependent and unable to live independently. If you're on a standard one-year renewable work or study permit, bringing parents typically isn't available yet — this becomes possible later, once you've built up permanent residence status.

## The Process, Step by Step

### Step 1: Confirm Your Eligibility
Once you've held your Bulgarian residence permit for at least one year (or immediately if you hold an EU Blue Card), you can begin the process.

### Step 2: Apply with the Local Migration Directorate in Bulgaria
As the sponsor, you submit the family reunification application to the Migration Directorate covering your registered address in Bulgaria. You'll need to show:
- Your valid Bulgarian residence permit
- Proof of adequate accommodation for your family member(s)
- Proof of sufficient financial resources to support them
- Proof of health insurance coverage for your family member(s)

Approval commonly takes **around one month**, though more complex cases can take longer.

### Step 3: Legalize Your Family Member's Documents from Nepal
This is the step that trips people up. Nepal is not part of the Hague Apostille Convention, and **Bulgaria has no embassy or consulate in Kathmandu** — the nearest is the **Bulgarian Embassy in New Delhi, India**, which also holds jurisdiction over Nepal. This means your family member's civil documents (marriage certificate, birth certificates for children, and any required police clearance) generally need to go through a specific chain:

1. Document issued in Nepal (marriage/birth certificate, police clearance, etc.)
2. Attestation by Nepal's **Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MOFA)** in Kathmandu
3. Certification by the **Embassy of Nepal in New Delhi, India**
4. Final certification by the **Bulgarian Embassy in New Delhi**

This is a multi-step, multi-country process that takes real coordination — it's exactly the kind of thing we manage on your behalf so your documents arrive properly legalized the first time, without delays or rejected paperwork.

### Step 4: Apply for the Family "D" Visa
Once your Migration Directorate application is approved and your documents are legalized, your family member applies in person for a **Type D (long-stay) visa** at the Bulgarian Embassy in New Delhi. Processing for this typically runs **around 4 to 6 weeks**, though it can vary.

### Step 5: Travel to Bulgaria and Register
Once the D visa is issued, your family member travels to Bulgaria. If they'll be staying more than six months, they'll need to register for a residence permit with the Migration Directorate after arrival — this step is typically processed within **10 to 14 working days**.

## Realistic Timeline

Putting the full chain together — sponsor eligibility confirmation, Migration Directorate approval, document legalization through New Delhi, D-visa processing, and final residence registration — a **realistic end-to-end estimate is around 3 to 4 months**, assuming documents are prepared correctly and there are no complications. Some straightforward cases move faster; document legalization delays from Nepal are the most common cause of a longer timeline, which is exactly why getting that step right from the start matters.
