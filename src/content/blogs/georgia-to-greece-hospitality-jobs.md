# From Georgia to Greece: Internship, Long-Stay D Visa & Seasonal Work Routes for Hospitality Jobs

## Introduction

If you're a student or young worker in Georgia looking at Greece for your next move, you're looking in the right direction. Greece's tourism industry runs on seasonal and entry-level hospitality staff — hotels, resorts, restaurants, and beach clubs across the islands and mainland need workers every year, and there are several legal routes in depending on your situation: an **internship visa**, a **long-stay National (Type D) visa**, or the **seasonal work visa** under Greece's newly updated Law 5275/2026.

As a Georgian citizen, you already benefit from visa-free short stays in the Schengen Area — but that only covers tourism-style visits up to 90 days. Working or interning in Greece long-term requires one of the specific visa routes below, applied for through the Greek Embassy in Tbilisi. This guide breaks down each route, what the hospitality jobs actually pay, and what to expect from the process.

---

## Why Greece for Georgian Students and Workers?

- **A resident Greek embassy in Tbilisi** handles visa applications directly — no need to travel to a third country for your consular appointment.
- **Genuine, high-volume hospitality demand.** Greece's tourism sector relies heavily on foreign seasonal and entry-level staff across hotels, resorts, and restaurants, particularly on the islands and coastal regions.
- **Multiple legal pathways** depending on your circumstances — student/internship, seasonal contract work, or longer-stay employment — rather than a single rigid route.
- **Accommodation-inclusive roles.** Many hospitality employers, especially in seasonal resort settings, include staff housing and meals as part of the package, which significantly increases the real value of the salary.

## Understanding Your Visa Options

### Option 1: Internship Visa (National D Visa – Internship Category)
If you have a confirmed internship placement with a Greek company (including in hospitality, tourism, or related fields), you can apply for a National (Type D) visa under the internship category. You'll need:
- Confirmation of the internship placement from the host organization
- Proof of relevant insurance coverage during your placement (public liability and occupational accident insurance, depending on your arrangement)
- Standard visa documents (passport, application form, financial proof)

This route suits students or recent graduates entering a structured internship program rather than standard paid employment.

### Option 2: Seasonal Work Visa (Under Law 5275/2026)
Greece's seasonal work visa was significantly overhauled under **Law 5275/2026**, and it's now one of the clearer routes into hospitality work for non-EU nationals. Key points:
- Covers **agriculture, tourism, hospitality, and food processing**
- Allows employment for **up to 9 months within a 12-month period**
- The process is **employer-driven**: a registered Greek employer files a digital invitation/approval request before you apply for your visa
- You must apply for the National (Type D) visa through the Greek Embassy in Tbilisi once your employer's application is approved
- The seasonal visa does not automatically convert into long-term residence — it's designed for defined-season employment, not permanent relocation

### Option 3: Long-Stay National (Type D) Visa for Standard Employment
For hospitality roles structured as standard (non-seasonal) employment contracts, the employer sponsors your work authorization, and you apply for the National D visa in the same way — through the Greek Embassy in Tbilisi — before traveling.

**Which one applies to you depends entirely on how your specific job offer is structured** — our team will confirm the right visa category once you have a confirmed placement.

## Job Deep Dive: Hospitality Roles in Greece

**Typical salary:** Starting from **€900/month, with accommodation and meals included** — a significant real-value increase over the base cash figure, since housing is usually the largest expense in Greece's tourist regions.

**Common roles:** hotel and resort staff, housekeeping, kitchen assistants, waitstaff, front-of-house and reception support, beach club and restaurant service roles.

**What employers typically look for:**
- Willingness to work seasonal, shift-based hours, including weekends and holidays — standard across Greek hospitality
- Basic English (often sufficient at entry level; additional languages are a plus in tourist-heavy areas)
- Prior hospitality or customer service experience is helpful but not always mandatory for entry-level roles

**Documents seasonal and hospitality employers commonly require:**
- Valid passport with at least 6 months' validity beyond your contract end date
- Health insurance covering your stay
- Police clearance certificate from Georgia
- Medical certificate confirming fitness for work
- Proof of accommodation in Greece (often provided directly by the employer)

## Practical Steps to Apply

1. **Secure a confirmed placement or job offer** — internship, seasonal, or standard employment — with a Greek employer or host organization. This is the starting point for every visa route above.
2. **Employer/host organization initiates the sponsorship process** on their end (digital invitation for seasonal roles, or direct sponsorship documentation for internships and standard employment).
3. **Apply for your National (Type D) visa** at the Greek Embassy in Tbilisi, with your full document set (passport, insurance, police clearance, medical certificate, proof of accommodation, and the placement/employment confirmation).
4. **Travel to Greece** once your visa is approved, and complete any required local registration on arrival (residence registration, AFM tax number if applicable).
5. **Start work** — only once your visa/permit is properly in place; starting before approval can jeopardize both your and the employer's standing.

---

## A Note on Timelines

Visa processing at Greek consulates commonly takes **several weeks to a few months**, depending on the visa category and season — internship and seasonal applications can move relatively efficiently when the employer-side documentation is submitted promptly and completely, but always build in buffer time rather than booking non-refundable travel before your visa is confirmed.
