# Solving Cyprus's Hospitality Labor Shortage: How to Recruit Qualified Non-EU Staff from Nepal

## Executive Summary

Cyprus hospitality runs on a tight seasonal calendar. Demand builds fast, peak weeks leave no room for gaps, and the local labor pool has not kept pace. For hotel operators, the result is familiar: **unfilled housekeeping shifts, understaffed kitchens and thin front-of-house teams** at the exact moments revenue is highest.

Many Cypriot employers now look to third-country (non-EU) nationals to close these gaps, working within the employment framework overseen by the Cyprus Ministry of Labour. The question is no longer *whether* to recruit internationally, but **how to do it reliably, compliantly and at scale**.

**Recruitly Group** is an international recruitment agency that deploys skilled, pre-vetted workers from Nepal to European enterprises. This guide explains the problem, why Nepali hospitality professionals are a strong fit, and how a structured, end-to-end process reduces hiring risk.

## The Problem: Open Positions Are an Operating Cost

A vacant role is not a neutral line on a staffing sheet. Every open position creates a chain of costs across the property.

### Where the gaps hit hardest

- **Housekeeping:** Slower room turnaround, delayed check-ins, and guest complaints about cleanliness.
- **Kitchen assistants and line cooks:** Pressure on the pass, reduced menu capacity and burned-out core teams.
- **F&B front-of-house:** Longer service times, lost covers and weaker guest reviews.

### The compounding effect

- **Overtime and burnout:** Existing staff cover the shortfall, raising labor costs and increasing resignations.
- **Revenue left on the table:** Restaurants close sections and rooms stay out of inventory when teams are short.
- **Reputation risk:** Service inconsistency feeds directly into online ratings, which drive future bookings.
- **Planning uncertainty:** Short notice hiring makes it hard to commit to season-long capacity.

For HR Directors and Operations Managers, the pattern repeats every season. Reactive, last-minute hiring rarely produces stable teams.

## Why Cyprus Employers Look to Third-Country Nationals

Local and EU labor supply for entry-level and mid-skill hospitality roles is limited relative to seasonal demand. Employers hiring third-country nationals must follow the procedures set by the Cyprus Ministry of Labour and related migration authorities, which govern approvals, permits and documentation.

**Important:** Rules, quotas and procedures can change. Employers should always confirm current requirements directly with the relevant Cypriot authorities. A recruitment partner familiar with the process helps you prepare accurate documentation and avoid avoidable delays.
