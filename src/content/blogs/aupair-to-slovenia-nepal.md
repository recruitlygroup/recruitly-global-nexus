# From Au Pair to Long-Term Worker in Slovenia: A Nepali Guide to Nanny, Babysitting & Hospitality Jobs

## Introduction

If you're a Nepali au pair currently based in the Netherlands, Austria, or Belgium, you already know the au pair route has a built-in expiry date — most placements are capped at one or two years, and then you're left figuring out what's next. For many Nepali workers, the answer is **Slovenia**: a smaller, calmer EU country with real long-term demand for childcare workers and hospitality staff, and a legal pathway that turns a temporary placement into a genuine long-term work and residence permit.

This isn't a freedom-of-movement shortcut — as a non-EU citizen, moving from one EU country's au pair scheme into long-term employment in another EU country still requires a proper work and residence permit process. But it's a well-established, achievable one, and this guide walks through exactly how it works: the job market, the salary ranges, and — critically — the document legalization chain (PCC, MOFA, embassy legalization) that trips up most first-time applicants.

## Why Slovenia (and Greece)?

Slovenia has a growing, steady need for household childcare workers and hospitality staff, particularly in and around Ljubljana and the country's tourism-heavy regions (Lake Bled, the Julian Alps, the Adriatic coast). For Nepali workers already living in Central Europe as au pairs, it offers:

- **A shorter transition than starting from Nepal.** You're already in the EU, already used to European household or hospitality work culture, and often already have relevant experience employers value.
- **Genuine long-term roles**, not another fixed-term au pair placement — nanny, babysitter, and hospitality positions in Slovenia are typically structured as ongoing employment contracts.
- **Manageable cost of living** relative to Western Europe, with steady demand in the tourism and hospitality sector year-round.
- **A regulated legal pathway.** Non-EU workers are sponsored by a registered Slovenian employer for a **single permit** (combined work and residence permit) — a formal process, not an informal arrangement.

## Job Deep Dive: Nanny, Babysitter & Hospitality Roles

### Nanny / Babysitter Roles
**Typical net salary:** €800–€1,200/month net, depending on live-in vs. live-out arrangements, number of children, and hours expected.

**What families typically look for:**
- Prior childcare experience (au pair experience counts strongly in your favor)
- Basic first aid knowledge (a certificate is a strong plus, not always mandatory)
- Basic English or willingness to learn simple Slovenian phrases for daily household communication
- References from your current or previous au pair placement

**Working arrangement:**
- Live-in positions often include free accommodation and meals as part of the package, which meaningfully changes your effective take-home value
- Live-out roles pay somewhat more in cash but require you to cover your own housing
- Hours vary by family but are typically structured around a standard working week, with overtime for evening or weekend childcare

### Hospitality Roles (Hotels, Restaurants, Resorts)
**Typical net salary:** €900–€1,300/month net, with seasonal roles (especially along the coast or ski regions) sometimes including tips and service charges on top.

**Common positions:** housekeeping, kitchen assistant, waitstaff, front-of-house support.

**What's typically required:**
- No formal certification needed for entry-level roles; hospitality experience from your home country or current placement is a plus
- Willingness to work shift patterns including weekends and holidays — this is standard in tourism-driven hospitality
- Basic language skills (English is usually sufficient at entry level; Slovenian helps for advancement)

## The Document Legalization Process: PCC, MOFA & Embassy Legalization

This is the part most applicants underestimate — and the part where delays most often happen. Since Nepal is not part of the Hague Apostille Convention, your Police Clearance Certificate (PCC) has to go through a specific attestation chain before Slovenia will accept it.

### Step 1: Obtain Your PCC from Nepal Police
Apply through Nepal Police's Online Police Clearance Registration (OPCR) system, or through a Nepali embassy if you're applying from abroad (a nominated representative in Nepal with a notarized power of attorney can also apply on your behalf). Processing generally takes a few days to a few weeks depending on the method.

### Step 2: MOFA Attestation (Kathmandu)
Your PCC then needs to be attested by Nepal's **Department of Consular Services, Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MOFA)** in Kathmandu. This can typically be done through MOFA's online attestation portal, and is a required step before any embassy will legalize the document — this attestation is what makes the certificate usable outside Nepal at all.

### Step 3: Embassy Legalization
After MOFA attestation, the certificate generally needs to be legalized by the destination country's embassy. Here's where it gets specific to your situation:

- **Nepal's own embassy that covers Slovenia is located in Vienna, Austria** — useful to know since many of our candidates are already based in Austria for their au pair placement.
- **Slovenia's embassy accredited to Nepal is officially based in New Delhi, India** — but since you're living in the EU (Austria, Netherlands, or Belgium) rather than in Nepal, you may be able to submit your visa and document legalization through the **Slovenian Embassy in Vienna** based on your legal residence in Austria. This isn't guaranteed for every applicant or every document type, so **confirm directly with the Slovenian Embassy in Vienna before assuming this applies to you.**

## Practical Relocation Steps

### Step 1: Secure a Confirmed Job Offer
Before anything else, you need a written job offer from a Slovenian employer (a family for nanny/babysitting roles, or a hospitality business). The employer initiates your **single permit** application through Slovenia's Employment Service — this cannot start without a confirmed offer.

### Step 2: Begin Document Legalization Early
Start your PCC and MOFA attestation process as soon as you have a job offer in hand — this is the step most likely to cause delays, so don't wait until your current au pair placement is ending.

### Step 3: Notify Your Current Host Family/Agency
Check your current au pair contract's notice period and any obligations around ending your placement early. Handle this transition cleanly — you may need a reference from your host family for your Slovenia application.

### Step 4: Apply for Your Visa
Once your single permit is approved and your legalized documents are in hand, apply for the appropriate entry visa. If you're transitioning directly from Austria, ask specifically whether you can process this through the Slovenian Embassy in Vienna given your residence status.

### Step 5: Register in Slovenia
On arrival, register your address at the local **upravna enota**, obtain your **EMŠO** and **davčna številka** (tax number), register with **ZZZS** for health insurance, and open a Slovenian bank account. Your employer typically assists with the ZZZS registration as part of onboarding.

### Step 6: Settle Into Accommodation
Live-in nanny/babysitter roles usually include housing as part of the arrangement. Hospitality employers frequently offer staff accommodation as well — confirm this in writing before you commit.
