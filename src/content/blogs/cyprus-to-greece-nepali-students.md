# From Cyprus to Greece: A Nepali Student's Guide to Hospitality Jobs and the National D Visa

## Introduction

If you're a Nepali student currently studying in Cyprus, Greece is a natural next step — geographically close, culturally familiar, and consistently short on hospitality staff across its hotels, resorts, and restaurants. As a non-EU national, moving from Cyprus to Greece isn't automatic (Cyprus residence doesn't carry EU-wide work rights), but there's a genuine advantage in your situation: **you can apply for your Greek visa directly from Cyprus**, without needing to travel back to Nepal.

This guide walks through exactly how that works, what hospitality roles in Greece typically pay, and the specific visa route that fits a student profile like yours.

---

## Why This Move Makes Sense From Cyprus Specifically

- **You apply locally, not from Nepal.** Greek consular rules require third-country nationals applying for a national long-stay visa to apply at the Greek diplomatic mission with jurisdiction over wherever they **legally reside** — for you, that's the **Greek Embassy in Nicosia**, since you hold a valid Cyprus residence permit. This saves you the cost and hassle of returning to Nepal to apply.
- **Nepal also maintains consular representation in Nicosia**, so if you need any Nepal-side documentation (like a fresh Police Clearance Certificate request or notarized documents) while you're still in Cyprus, that's accessible locally too.
- **Genuine hospitality demand in Greece.** Hotels, resorts, and restaurants — particularly on the islands and coastal regions — rely heavily on seasonal and entry-level non-EU staff.
- **Cultural and logistical proximity.** Cyprus to Greece is a short, direct flight, making the transition far more manageable than relocating from further afield.

## Which Visa Route Fits a Student Profile?

### Option 1: Internship Visa (National D Visa – Internship Category)
If you can secure a structured internship placement with a Greek hospitality employer (or a related tourism business), you apply for a National (Type D) visa under the internship category. You'll typically need:
- Confirmation of the internship placement from the host organization
- Occupational accident and public liability insurance coverage for your placement
- Standard visa documents (passport, application form, proof of funds)

This is often the most natural fit if you're still enrolled as a student and want placement-based experience rather than open-ended employment.

### Option 2: Seasonal Work Visa (Under Law 5275/2026)
If your placement is with a hospitality employer for a fixed season (a common structure for summer resort or hotel roles), Greece's updated seasonal work visa framework applies:
- Covers **tourism, hospitality, agriculture, and food processing**
- Permits work for **up to 9 months within a 12-month period**
- **Employer-driven**: the Greek employer files a digital invitation/approval request before you apply for your visa
- You then apply for the National (Type D) visa at the Greek Embassy in Nicosia, based on your Cyprus residence

### Option 3: Standard Employment (Long-Stay National D Visa)
For hospitality roles structured as ongoing (non-seasonal) employment, the employer sponsors your work authorization directly, and the same National D visa application process applies through the Greek Embassy in Nicosia.

**Which route fits you depends on how your specific job or internship offer is structured** — we'll help confirm the right category once you have a placement.

## Job Deep Dive: Hospitality Roles in Greece

**Typical salary:** Entry-level hospitality roles for seasonal or student placements commonly start in a similar range to other non-EU hospitality hires across Greece — often with **accommodation and meals included**, which meaningfully increases the real value of the position given housing costs in Greece's tourist regions.

**Common roles:** hotel and resort staff, housekeeping, kitchen assistants, waitstaff, front-of-house and reception support.

**What employers typically look for:**
- Willingness to work shift-based, seasonal hours, including weekends and holidays
- Basic English (usually sufficient at entry level)
- Some hospitality or customer-facing experience is a plus, though not always mandatory for entry-level roles

**Documents commonly required:**
- Valid passport with at least 6 months' validity beyond your contract end date
- Health insurance covering your stay
- Police clearance certificate (confirm with the Greek Embassy in Nicosia whether a fresh Nepal-issued certificate is required or whether your existing Cyprus documentation is accepted)
- Medical certificate confirming fitness for work
- Proof of accommodation in Greece (often arranged directly by the employer)

## Practical Steps to Apply

1. **Secure a confirmed internship or job offer** with a Greek hospitality employer or host organization.
2. **Employer/host organization initiates sponsorship** on their end — a digital invitation for seasonal roles, or direct placement/employment documentation for internships and standard roles.
3. **Apply for your National (Type D) visa at the Greek Embassy in Nicosia**, using your Cyprus residence permit as the basis for applying locally rather than from Nepal.
4. **Travel to Greece** once your visa is approved, and complete local registration on arrival (residence registration, AFM tax number if applicable).
5. **Start work only once your visa/permit is confirmed** — starting before approval risks both your standing and the employer's.

---

## A Note on Timelines

Visa processing timelines vary by category and season, and Greece's seasonal work framework under Law 5275/2026 is still relatively new, so processing experiences may still be settling into a consistent pattern. Build in buffer time, and avoid booking non-refundable travel before your visa is confirmed.
