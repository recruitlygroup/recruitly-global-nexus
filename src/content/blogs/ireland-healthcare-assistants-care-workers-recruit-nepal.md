# Ireland Care Worker Shortage 2026: How Nursing Homes and Home Care Providers Can Hire Healthcare Assistants from Nepal

## Executive Summary

Ireland's care sector depends on healthcare assistants, home carers and care workers. They support older people in nursing homes, clients in their own homes and people in disability services. Demand continues to outpace the domestic supply of workers.

Healthcare is the sector that uses Ireland's employment permit system more than any other, and the government has written specific rules for lower-paid care roles, including a dedicated salary threshold. For providers, this means a **clear legal route exists**, but success depends on understanding the permit conditions, vetting requirements and realistic timelines.

## The Problem: Care Gaps Are Felt Immediately

### What shortages mean for care providers

- **Reduced capacity:** Nursing homes and home care services cannot accept new clients without enough staff.
- **Strain on existing teams:** Overtime and short staffing raise burnout and turnover.
- **Continuity of care:** Clients benefit from consistent carers, and high churn makes that difficult.
- **Compliance risk:** Staffing ratios and care standards require adequate, trained teams.

### Why recruitment from abroad is part of the answer

Ireland's population is ageing, and care needs are growing. Government and industry have acknowledged that the domestic labor market cannot fill every care vacancy, and the permit system is designed to let employers recruit from outside the EEA where they cannot find workers locally.

## How Hiring Non-EEA Care Workers Works in Ireland

### The permit route

Healthcare assistants, home carers and care workers are hired through the **General Employment Permit (GEP)**, with specific conditions for these roles. The role must be correctly classified, and employers must meet the remuneration and employer requirements.

### Salary threshold

From **1 March 2026**, the Department of Enterprise, Tourism and Employment (DETE) set a separate minimum annual remuneration of **€32,691** for healthcare assistants, home carers and care workers (and meat and horticultural operatives). This compares with **€36,605** for a standard General Employment Permit. Under the published roadmap, the gap between the two is intended to close by 2030, and thresholds will continue to rise. Applications below the threshold are refused.

### Quotas and the occupation lists

Care Worker and Home Carer roles have operated under a **quota system** for General Employment Permits, and the quota was extended by a further 1,000 permits in March 2025. Occupation lists and quotas were reviewed again in May 2026. **Always check the current DETE position on quotas and eligibility** before planning a recruitment round.

### Other key requirements

- **Labour Market Needs Test (LMNT):** Where required, the employer must advertise the role, and advertisements published from 1 March 2026 must comply with the revised salary thresholds.
- **The 50/50 rule:** In general, at least half of an employer's workforce must be EEA nationals. Confirm how it applies to your organization.
- **Training qualification:** Recruitment guides report that healthcare assistants are expected to hold a **QQI Level 5 healthcare support qualification** or an equivalent. Confirm the current requirement with DETE and, where relevant, HIQA and your registered provider standards.
- **Garda vetting:** Roles working with older people or vulnerable adults require vetting through the National Vetting Bureau. The employer arranges this.

**Important:** Thresholds, quotas and conditions change regularly. Verify current requirements with DETE before submitting any application. Only the employer can apply for the permit.

### Nurses are a different route

Registered nurses require registration with the Nursing and Midwifery Board of Ireland (NMBI) and follow different rules. This guide covers care assistant and care worker roles only.
