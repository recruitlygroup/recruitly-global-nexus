# MBBS in Germany 2027-28: Fees, German-Language Rules, Admission & Reality Check for Nepali, Indian, Gulf, Filipino, Indonesian & Lebanese Students

**Quick answer:** There is no "MBBS" in Germany. Medicine is a roughly **6-year-and-3-month** single programme that ends with the **Staatsexamen (state examination)** and leads to the German licence, the **Approbation**. Public universities teach it **in German**, charge **no tuition in most states** (Baden-Württemberg charges non-EU students **€1,500 per semester**), and admit very few non-EU applicants. The only fully English-taught route we found in Germany is the **UMFST-UMCH campus in Hamburg**, which charges **€17,400 per semester** for non-EU students.

Germany is a strong choice if you want to **live and work in Germany or the EU** and you are prepared to learn medical German. It is a poor choice if your plan is a cheap, English-medium degree to take home. The rest of this guide explains why, with figures checked against official and news sources (listed at the end).

## The reality check: no MBBS, and no English at public universities

Search results for "MBBS in Germany" can mislead you, so start with three facts:

- **Germany does not award an MBBS.** Medicine is a single programme (Humanmedizin) of about **six years and three months**, completed by a state examination. The state examination is the precondition for the **Approbation**, the licence to practise.
- **Public medical education is taught in German only.** DAAD-published guidance states that medical education is provided in German, and the programme requires interaction with patients in German.
- **Applications are very competitive.** The same DAAD guidance notes that neither DAAD nor other German organisations offer scholarships for foreign students of medicine.

If an agent promises you an English-medium, easy-entry "MBBS in Germany" at a public university, ask for the university's own admission page.

## The two routes: German-taught public vs English-taught private

### Route 1: German-taught public universities

- **Tuition:** usually €0, with exceptions by state (see [fees](#mbbs-in-germany-fees-and-the-real-cost)).
- **Language:** German, typically at **C1** (for example TestDaF 4x4, DSH-2 or Goethe C1) before you start university medicine.
- **Admission for non-EU applicants:** limited reserved quota, very competitive.
- **Degree and licence:** Staatsexamen and Approbation.

### Route 2: English-taught private campus (UMFST-UMCH, Hamburg)

- **What it is:** a branch campus in Hamburg of the Romanian **George Emil Palade University of Medicine, Pharmacy, Science and Technology of Târgu Mureș**. It offers a 6-year medicine programme in English, and the university says the Hamburg programme was accredited by IAAR in 2023 and that the university is listed in the World Directory of Medical Schools (WDOMS).
- **Official tuition (2026):** **€17,400 per semester for non-EU applicants** (€14,900 for EU/EEA/UK/Swiss applicants), plus a one-time **€595 application fee** and **€4,500 enrolment fee**. From the third year, students can choose to continue in Târgu Mureș at an advertised **€3,000 per semester**.
- **Degree:** a **Romanian university** degree, not a German Staatsexamen.
- **Important caveats:** some admissions sources say German is used during clinical training. Whether a UMCH graduate can obtain the German Approbation, and under which procedure, depends on how the German authorities treat the degree and your nationality. **Get this confirmed in writing before you pay.**

> **Fee warning:** one competitor guide quotes UMCH at "€14,000 to €15,500 per semester." The university's own page lists **€14,900 for EU applicants and €17,400 for everyone else**. For Nepali, Indian, Gulf, Filipino, Indonesian and Lebanese students the €17,400 figure applies.

## How non-EU students actually get in

Non-EU applicants follow a different path from German and EU applicants.

1. **A separate application channel.** Applicants with a secondary school certificate from outside the EU generally **apply directly to the university or through uni-assist**, not through the central Hochschulstart system used by EU applicants.
2. **A reserved quota of about 5%.** Reports and university pages describe about **5% of places** at each faculty reserved for non-EU applicants (for example, the University of Freiburg states this on its own pages).
3. **Selection mainly on school grades.** For non-EU applicants, selection is primarily based on your secondary-school results, and many guides say the aptitude test (TMS) is mainly used by EU applicants. **Check each university**, since some may use TestAS or other tests. We could not find a single published universal grade cut-off, so treat "you need exactly 1.0" claims with caution. Competition is intense and cut-offs vary by university and year.
4. **Studienkolleg for most students from 12-year school systems.** Students whose school certificate is not recognised as equivalent to the German Abitur (commonly including India and Nepal, with Class 12 alone) usually need a **one-year Studienkolleg** (the medical track is the **M-Kurs**) and must pass the **Feststellungsprüfung**. Entry usually requires German at about **B1-B2**. One year of successful university study in your home country can change the route in some cases, so check the DAAD admission database.
5. **APS certificate (India only, not Nepal).** Indian applicants need the **APS certificate** (Akademische Prüfstelle) for university and Studienkolleg applications. **Nepali applicants do not need APS**, despite some consultancies saying they do. Confirm in the DAAD database.
6. **Deadlines.** Winter-semester deadlines are commonly around **15 July** via uni-assist, but each university sets its own, and uni-assist processing can take several weeks.

## MBBS in Germany fees and the real cost

### 1. Tuition by state (public universities)

- **Baden-Württemberg:** **€1,500 per semester** for most non-EU students at public universities (Heidelberg, Freiburg, Tübingen, Mannheim, Ulm and others), in force since winter semester 2017/18. Some exemptions exist.
- **Bavaria:** universities decide individually. **TUM** charges non-EU students from about €2,000-6,000 per semester depending on the programme, and one tracker reports that **FAU** will charge new non-EU students from summer semester 2027. **LMU** is reported to charge no tuition for regular programmes. Check each Bavarian medical faculty's current fee page.
- **Other states** (including Berlin, Hamburg, Hesse, Lower Saxony, North Rhine-Westphalia and Saxony): no general non-EU tuition in 2026. Some states are debating changes, so verify before you apply.

### 2. Semester contribution (all students)

Every student pays a **semester contribution**, roughly **€70-430 per semester** depending on the university. It is not tuition and often includes a transport ticket.

### 3. The visa financial proof is not a fee

For a standard 2026 student visa, you must show **€11,904 for one year (€992 per month)**, usually through a **blocked account** (Sperrkonto). The amount follows the BAföG rate and has been unchanged since 2024. It is **proof of living funds**, not a payment to the university, and it must not be double-counted with your living budget.

### 4. Illustrative total budgets

These are planning scenarios, not quotes. They use the €992 monthly figure as a baseline for living costs, which is often lower than real costs in expensive cities, and exclude flights, language courses and one-off costs.

| Scenario | What it assumes | Approximate tuition | Living at €992/month |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Public university, tuition-free state** | 1 year Studienkolleg + about 6.25 years of medicine; semester contributions extra | €0 | About €86,000 |
| **B. Public university, Baden-Württemberg** | As A, plus €1,500 for each of about 12 semesters | Up to about €18,000 | About €86,000 |
| **C. UMCH Hamburg, full programme** | 12 semesters at €17,400 + €595 + €4,500 | About €213,900 | About €71,000 (6 years) |
| **D. UMCH, Hamburg then Târgu Mureș (as advertised)** | 4 semesters at €17,400, then 8 semesters at €3,000, plus fees | About €98,700 | Varies by city |

**Conversion note:** published euro-to-rupee conversions in 2026 range from roughly ₹10.6 lakh to ₹13 lakh for the €11,904 blocked amount depending on the date, rate and charges used. Convert at the **live rate** when you plan.

**Other costs to budget:** German courses and exams, document translation and attestation, application fees, health insurance, accommodation deposits, flights, and licensing-exam preparation after graduation.

## What a German medical degree means in your country

Your home regulator decides whether you can practise at home, not Germany. The sections below summarise the main points; confirm everything with your regulator **in writing before you enrol**.

### India

- **NEET qualification is required** under NMC rules.
- India's **FMGL Regulations, 2021** require, among other conditions, **English as the medium of instruction** (Regulation 4(a)(iii)), at least **54 months** of study and a **12-month internship in the same institution**.
- **German-taught public programmes are therefore a serious risk for anyone planning to return to India.** We found no NMC ruling that specifically addresses German-taught degrees, but the regulation's wording asks for English. Do not assume an exception.
- **Open questions even for English-taught options:** whether the NMC treats a UMCH degree (a Romanian degree with some German-language clinical training) as compliant, and whether Germany's **Praktisches Jahr** (the final clinical year inside the course) is accepted as the 12-month internship. **Ask the NMC and the university in writing.**
- Graduates still need the **FMGE** (NExT remains deferred) and a 12-month supervised internship in India. The FMGE pass rate was about **12-13%** in June 2026 (about 4,635 of roughly 36,000-37,000 candidates).
- Indian students also need the **APS certificate** to apply to Germany.

If your goal is to practise in **India**, Germany is generally not the efficient route. See our [MBBS in Georgia guide](/blog/mbbs-in-georgia-2027-guide-international-students) for a different set of trade-offs, including an unresolved NMC question there.

### Nepal

- Nepal's Medical Education Commission requires Nepali students going abroad to pass its entrance exam and obtain an **eligibility letter / NOC** before enrolling in a foreign medical programme. Check the current notice and the Ministry of Education's NOC portal.
- To practise in Nepal you need **Nepal Medical Council recognition and registration** and must pass the **Nepal Medical Council Licensing Examination (NMCLE)**. Ask the Council, in writing, how it treats the specific German university and degree.
- **Nepali applicants do not need an APS certificate**, but with Class 12 alone you will typically need a **Studienkolleg**.
- NEET and FMGE do not apply to Nepali students.

### Gulf countries (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Qatar, Oman, Bahrain)

- There is **no NEET or FMGE** in your route, but each regulator (in the UAE, the Dubai Health Authority, Department of Health Abu Dhabi and the Ministry of Health and Prevention) sets its own rules.
- The UAE's unified framework requires graduation from a recognised, accredited medical school, a **12-month internship**, primary-source verification and a licensing exam. Whether your regulator counts Germany's Praktisches Jahr as the internship is a question to ask.
- **We did not find any official statement on how Gulf regulators treat German-taught or UMCH degrees.** Ask your target regulator, and if you hold a government scholarship, ask your ministry of higher education about equivalency **before** applying.

### Philippines

- Filipino citizens with a foreign medical degree need a **CHED Certificate of Equivalency** for the degree (and for the postgraduate internship) before sitting the **Physician Licensure Examination** run by the PRC.
- Philippine medical programmes normally follow a bachelor's degree. Ask CHED in writing whether a German single-cycle degree can be certified as equivalent and whether any pre-medical or bachelor's-level requirement applies.

### Indonesia

- Indonesian citizens who graduate in medicine abroad must complete Indonesia's **adaptation and competency-evaluation process** under the Ministry of Health, starting with degree equivalence. Rules have been revised in recent years, and published reforms focus mainly on specialists.
- Confirm the current process for a general-practitioner degree with **Kemenkes (adaptasi.kemkes.go.id)** before enrolling.

### Lebanon

- Foreign degrees go to the **Equivalence Committee at the Ministry of Education and Higher Education (MEHE)**, followed by Lebanon's **colloquium exam**, licensing with the Ministry of Public Health and registration with the **Order of Physicians**.
- Lebanese rules have historically placed **special conditions on studying medicine abroad** (a merit exam and an evaluation exam after the second year under Decree 9276). Confirm with MEHE which conditions apply to you today.

## If you want to work in Germany: Approbation and staying on

For many international students, this is the real reason to choose Germany.

- **German graduates receive the Approbation** after passing the state examination, with no separate foreign-degree recognition. Because you studied in German, the language barrier for licensing is already behind you.
- **After graduation, you can apply for a residence permit of up to 18 months to look for a job** that matches your qualification (Section 20 of the Residence Act). It cannot be extended beyond 18 months, and employment is permitted during the search.
- **Why a German degree matters more than ever:** from **1 November 2026**, for applications by doctors with a degree from a **non-EU country**, the **Kenntnisprüfung** (a medical knowledge exam) became the **standard route** to the Approbation (section 9d of the Federal Medical Code), alongside the Fachsprachprüfung medical-German exam. Doctors who trained in Germany do not take the route designed for foreign degrees.
- **UMCH graduates:** the degree is Romanian. Reports describe EU-issued qualifications as recognised under EU rules, but how this applies to **non-EU nationals** holding an EU-issued degree is a point to confirm with the competent German authority in writing.

## Pros and cons of studying medicine in Germany

### Pros

- **No general tuition** in most states for public universities
- A **full German licence (Approbation)** on completion
- A route to **post-study residence** (up to 18 months) and a long-term career in German-speaking healthcare
- **Strong teaching hospitals** and research
- A single, regulated programme structure

### Cons

- **Medicine at public universities is taught only in German**, and you need advanced German before clinical study
- **Very competitive non-EU quota** (about 5% of places) with admission mainly based on school grades
- **A long pathway:** language, Studienkolleg and 6+ years of medicine
- **High living costs for 7+ years**, even with free tuition
- **English-taught option is expensive** and carries recognition questions
- **Difficult to use as a quick route home**, especially for India's English-medium rule
- **Rules change**, including state tuition and licensing reforms

## How to verify any programme in 15 minutes

1. **Check the university's own page** for the programme language, non-EU admission route and quota.
2. **Confirm the fee in writing**, including state tuition rules, semester contribution and any enrolment fee.
3. **Check the degree awarded** (German Staatsexamen or a foreign university's degree).
4. **Ask your home regulator in writing** (NMC, Nepal Medical Council, your Gulf regulator, CHED/PRC, Kemenkes, MEHE) whether it accepts this specific degree and what exams, equivalency or internship steps apply.
5. **Ask about licensing in Germany**: how graduates of this programme obtain the Approbation, and under which procedure, for your nationality.
6. **Check language requirements** and which certificates the university accepts.
7. **Speak to current students** from your country, not only agent-supplied testimonials.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there an MBBS in Germany?

No. German universities offer a single medicine programme (Humanmedizin) of about 6 years and 3 months, ending in the Staatsexamen and leading to the Approbation. It is not an MBBS or MD in the Indian or Georgian sense.

### Can I study medicine in Germany in English?

At public universities, no. The English-taught option we found is the UMFST-UMCH campus in Hamburg, which awards a Romanian university degree and charges €17,400 per semester to non-EU students.

### Is medicine in Germany free?

Tuition is free at most public universities outside Baden-Württemberg, but you must pay semester contributions and living costs, and Baden-Württemberg charges non-EU students €1,500 per semester. Bavarian universities set their own rules.

### Do I need German to study medicine in Germany?

Yes at public universities, usually at C1 level before clinical study, with B1-B2 typically required to enter a Studienkolleg.

### Do I need Studienkolleg?

Students from 12-year school systems (including many Indian and Nepali applicants with Class 12 alone) commonly do, but your route depends on your certificate and any university study. Check the DAAD admission database.

### Do Nepali students need an APS certificate for Germany?

No. APS applies to applicants from India, China and Vietnam. Confirm in the DAAD admission database.

### What is the blocked account amount for 2026?

€11,904 for one year (€992 per month) for a standard student visa. It is proof of funds, not a tuition payment.

### Will a German degree let me practise in India?

Probably not through the German-taught route. India's FMGL rules require English as the medium of instruction, and we found no NMC exception for German-taught programmes. Ask the NMC in writing before enrolling.

### Can I stay and work in Germany after graduating?

Graduates of German universities can apply for a residence permit of up to 18 months to look for a qualifying job, and doctors obtain the Approbation to practise.

### How much does it cost in total?

It depends on the route. At a tuition-free public university, living costs alone over the whole pathway can reach about €86,000 at the visa-proof rate. At UMCH, tuition alone is about €213,900 for the full programme at the non-EU rate.
