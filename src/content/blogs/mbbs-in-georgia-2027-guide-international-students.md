# MBBS in Georgia 2027: Complete Guide for Nepali, Indian, Gulf, Filipino, Indonesian & Lebanese Students

**Quick answer:** MBBS in Georgia (the Caucasus country, not the US state) is a 6-year, English-taught Doctor of Medicine (MD) programme offered to international students by **private universities**. Tuition is roughly **US$5,000-8,000 per year**, and a realistic all-in budget for the full course is **about US$50,000-65,000**. Since 2026-27, **state universities, including Tbilisi State Medical University, no longer admit new international students** (except in narrow exceptional cases).

The most important point in this guide is not the fee. **Whether a Georgian MD lets you practise in your own country depends on your home regulator, not on Georgia.** Each country below has different rules, and for Indian students there is a live regulatory question you must understand before paying a deposit.

Everything here was checked against news reports, government notices and regulator documents; sources are listed at the end.

## What changed for 2026-27

In December 2025, Georgia's Minister of Education, Science and Youth, Givi Mikanadze, announced that **state universities will stop admitting foreign students from the next academic year, except in narrowly defined exceptional cases.** International applicants are directed to private universities.

What this means for you:

- **Tbilisi State Medical University (TSMU) and other state universities are closed to new international applicants.** Officials said international students made up roughly 45% of TSMU's student body.
- **Many older blogs and agent brochures still list TSMU.** If a consultant offers you a state-university seat for 2027, ask for written confirmation of the "exceptional case."
- **Students already enrolled are not affected.**
- **The rule applies to every nationality**, not only one.

Policies can change again, so confirm each university's international admission status **in writing** before you pay.

## Is Georgia in the EU?

**No.** Georgia is not an EU member state.

- Georgia received **EU candidate status in December 2023**.
- The European Council and Commission have described its accession process as **de facto halted** since 2024, and the Commission's 2026 enlargement package repeated that, calling Georgia a candidate "in name only."
- A Georgian MD therefore does **not** give automatic EU recognition or work rights.

Choose Georgia for its cost, English-medium teaching and the licensing route that fits **your** country, not for EU membership.

## The licensing question nobody should skip

A Georgian degree has two separate layers:

1. **The degree itself:** a 6-year MD from a university accredited in Georgia (accreditation is handled by Georgia's National Center for Educational Quality Enhancement, NCEQE).
2. **Your right to practise at home:** decided by your own country's regulator, using its own rules, exams and paperwork.

Two cautions apply to every nationality:

- **Practising independently inside Georgia is a long road.** On graduation, MD holders are registered as **junior doctors**. Georgian authorities state that a certificate of specialist practice follows the postgraduate stage, and the Indian embassy responsible for Georgia has said that a full licence at par with citizens requires postgraduate training and Georgian-language knowledge. Plan on returning home (or to a third country) to practise.
- **"Recognised in X country" claims from consultants are often unverified.** Several agent websites state that Georgian degrees are "recognised" by named health authorities in the Gulf and elsewhere. Treat those statements as marketing until you have confirmed them with the regulator itself, in writing.

## What a Georgian MD means in your country

### Nepal

**Before you leave Nepal**

- Nepal's Medical Education Commission (MEC) requires Nepali students going abroad for medical degrees to **pass its entrance examination (MECEE-BL)** and obtain an **eligibility letter / No Objection Certificate (NOC)** through the official government process. MEC has published notices telling foreign universities to admit Nepali students only after verifying this letter, and warning that students who skip it carry the risk of non-recognition later.
- Check the current notice on the MEC website and the Ministry of Education's NOC portal (noc.moest.gov.np) before applying anywhere.

**After graduation**

- To practise in Nepal you must have your degree recognised and registered by the **Nepal Medical Council** and pass the **Nepal Medical Council Licensing Examination (NMCLE)**.
- Recognition is **not automatic for every foreign university**. Ask the Nepal Medical Council, in writing, how it treats the specific Georgian university and degree you are considering.

**What does not apply to you:** NEET and FMGE are Indian requirements.

### India

**Before you enrol**

- You need a **NEET UG qualification** to be eligible under NMC rules.
- Your course must meet India's **Foreign Medical Graduate Licentiate (FMGL) Regulations, 2021**: at least **54 months** of study, a **12-month internship in the same institution**, English as the medium of instruction, and the Schedule I subjects.

**After graduation**

- Clear the **FMGE** screening exam (NExT remains deferred, so check the NMC and NBEMS websites for the current position), complete a **12-month supervised internship in India**, then obtain permanent registration.

**The live regulatory question (read this carefully)**

Regulation 4(b) of the FMGL rules requires the graduate to be registered with the regulator of the country of study with a **right to practise "at par" with that country's own citizens.** In Georgia, new MD graduates are registered as **junior doctors**.

- In response to a grievance dated **24 September 2026**, the NMC said junior-doctor status satisfies Regulation 4(b) **only if it legally permits practice at the required level**; if independent practice in Georgia is allowed only after postgraduate certification, junior-doctor status alone would not satisfy the regulation. The NMC also said the determination depends on Georgian law and licensing requirements.
- On **14 October 2026**, Georgia's NCEQE issued a 17-point statement arguing that its six-year MD meets the FMGL requirements: that graduates receive a registration number with the right to work as junior doctors on the same terms as Georgian graduates, and that the scope of supervised or independent practice is a matter of each country's own law. Georgian authorities said they were placing documentation before Indian authorities.
- The Indian embassy covering Georgia had earlier advised that graduates work in a supervised, non-independent role and need postgraduate training for an independent licence.
- Business Standard has reported that roughly 20,000-25,000 Indian students are studying medicine in Georgia and that parents allege some consultants misled students about eligibility.

**As of 10 November 2026, we found no final NMC ruling resolving this question.** The NCEQE statement was circulated through a paid press release, so read it as Georgia's position, not as an Indian regulatory decision.

**What we recommend for Indian applicants**

1. Read the NMC's position and the NCEQE statement yourself (links in Sources).
2. Ask every consultant, **including us**, to explain in writing how the course satisfies Regulation 4(b), and what happens if the NMC rules against it.
3. Do not rely on forwarded posters or social-media screenshots from either side. Use nmc.org.in and the Gazette of India.
4. Decide with the risk in view, and keep a fallback plan (for example, an Indian seat or another destination).

**FMGE pass rates are low for everyone.** In June 2026, about **12-13%** of candidates passed (around 4,635 of roughly 36,000-37,000), while the previous session was around 23-25%. The health ministry told Parliament that first-time candidates passed at over 35%. NBEMS does not publish pass rates by university or country, so be sceptical of any university-specific figure that is not verifiable.

### Gulf countries (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Qatar, Oman, Bahrain)

There is **no NEET and no FMGE** in your route, but there is no single Gulf licence either. Each country (and in the UAE, each authority) sets its own requirements.

- **UAE:** the Dubai Health Authority, Department of Health Abu Dhabi and the Ministry of Health and Prevention use a unified framework. It requires graduation from a recognised, accredited medical school, a **12-month internship**, primary-source verification of your documents, and a licensing exam. Experience requirements apply for general practitioner posts, so check the current rules.
- **Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Qatar, Oman and Bahrain:** each regulator runs its own credential evaluation and licensing exam and sets its own experience and recognition conditions.
- **If you hold a government scholarship or need your ministry of higher education to equivalise the degree**, confirm that Georgian universities are accepted **before** enrolling.

Georgian authorities state that MD holders may seek licensing through the health authorities of the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Oman and Bahrain via the standard pathway for an MBBS-equivalent degree. That is Georgia's description of the pathway; **confirm the current requirements for your target country directly with its regulator.**

**Action:** shortlist the country where you want to work, then ask that regulator which Georgian universities it accepts and what exams, internship and experience it requires.

### Philippines

Filipino citizens with a foreign medical degree must have it recognised before sitting the **Physician Licensure Examination (PLE)** run by the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC).

- The PRC asks for a **CHED Certificate of Equivalency for the degree** and, for physicians, a **CHED equivalency for the postgraduate internship**.
- Philippine medical programmes are normally taken after a bachelor's degree. **Ask CHED in writing whether a direct-entry six-year Georgian MD can be certified as equivalent, and whether any additional pre-medical or bachelor's-level requirements would apply.** Do this before you pay a deposit.

### Indonesia

Indonesian citizens who graduate in medicine abroad must go through Indonesia's **adaptation and competency-evaluation process** under the Ministry of Health (Kemenkes) before they can practise, starting with degree equivalence through the education authorities.

- The adaptation rules have been revised in recent years, and the Health Minister has asked for faster, more transparent processes. Published reforms so far focus mainly on specialist doctors.
- **Confirm the current rules for a general-practitioner degree with Kemenkes (adaptasi.kemkes.go.id) before enrolling.**

### Lebanon

Lebanon relies on **degree equivalence and its own licensing steps**.

- Foreign degrees go to the **Equivalence Committee at the Ministry of Education and Higher Education (MEHE)**. Graduates then take Lebanon's **colloquium exam**, register with the **Ministry of Public Health** for a licence and join the **Order of Physicians**.
- Lebanese regulations have historically imposed **special conditions on students who study medicine abroad**, including a merit exam and an evaluation exam after the second year (Decree 9276). **Confirm with MEHE which conditions apply to you today.**

## Eligibility, admission, visa and residence permit

### Typical eligibility

- Secondary school completion with **Biology and Chemistry** (most universities also expect Physics and English); a minimum of about **50%** is commonly required
- Age **17** by the start of the programme (typical university rule)
- Any **home-country entrance requirement** (NEET for India; MECEE-BL and the NOC for Nepal) **before** you apply
- Valid passport and medical fitness

Requirements vary by university and nationality, so use the university's own list.

### Documents checklist

- Passport, school certificates and transcripts (apostille or attestation as required)
- Home-country exam/eligibility documents (for example, NEET scorecard or Nepal's eligibility letter)
- Birth certificate (English or officially translated)
- Photographs and medical fitness certificate
- Admission letter, proof of tuition payment and proof of funds
- Accommodation and travel details

### Step by step

1. **Confirm your home-country requirements and licensing route** (the section for your country above).
2. **Shortlist private universities** and confirm 2027 international admission status in writing.
3. **Apply** with academic documents and your home-country eligibility paperwork.
4. **Receive the admission letter.** Timelines vary by university.
5. **Pay the first-year fee directly to the university** and keep the receipt. Avoid personal accounts.
6. **Apply for the D3 (study) visa** through Georgia's official channels. Visa rules depend on your passport, so check the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Georgia (geoconsul.gov.ge).
7. **Travel to Georgia** and enrol.
8. **Apply for the study residence permit** at the Public Service Hall; the D3 visa is commonly cited as valid for 90 days, with the permit to be applied for within about 45 days of arrival.

Georgia has tightened study residence-permit rules recently, so check the latest guidance from the Public Service Development Agency before you travel. Register with your embassy or consulate on arrival.

## Pros and cons of MBBS in Georgia

### Pros

- **English-medium MD** designed for international students
- **Lower cost** than private medical education in many home countries
- **Accredited by Georgia's NCEQE**, which holds WFME recognition
- **Large international student community** in Tbilisi and Batumi
- **Compact, walkable cities** and a relatively simple admission process

### Cons

- **State universities are closed** to new international students, narrowing choice
- **Quality varies sharply** between private universities
- **Licensing at home is your responsibility**, and the rules differ by country; for Indian students there is currently an open question on Regulation 4(b)
- **Independent practice inside Georgia** requires postgraduate training and Georgian-language knowledge
- **Cold winters** and a different climate and food culture
- **One main intake per year**
- **Rules change.** Admission policy and residence-permit rules have moved quickly

## How to verify any university in 15 minutes

Do this for **every** shortlisted university, whoever recommends it:

1. **Check NCEQE accreditation** of the specific MD programme on eqe.ge.
2. **Search the university** in the **World Directory of Medical Schools** (WDOMS).
3. **Ask your home regulator, in writing,** whether it will accept the degree and what exams, internship and equivalency steps apply.
4. **Confirm 2027 international admission status** in writing.
5. **Get the full fee schedule in writing**, including hostel, insurance and any annual increase.
6. **Ask for the graduate outcomes that matter to you** (for example, licensing-exam results for students from your country), with numbers and years.
7. **Speak to current students from your country**, not only agent-supplied testimonials.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can international students still study MBBS in Georgia in 2027?

Yes, through **private universities**. State universities, including TSMU, stopped admitting new international students from 2026-27, except in narrowly defined exceptional cases.

### Is the Georgian MD the same as an MBBS?

Internationally, the MD is regarded as equivalent to MBBS. Whether your **home regulator** accepts it for licensing is a separate question that you must confirm with that regulator.

### Is NEET required for MBBS in Georgia?

For **Indian** students who want to practise in India, yes, under NMC rules. It is **not** a requirement for Nepali, Gulf, Filipino, Indonesian or Lebanese students, who must follow their own countries' rules.

### Do Nepali students need an entrance exam before going abroad?

Nepal's Medical Education Commission requires Nepali students to pass its entrance exam and obtain an eligibility letter / NOC before studying medicine abroad. Check the current MEC notice before applying.

### Will a Georgian MD let me practise in India?

That depends on NMC's FMGL 2021 rules and the FMGE. As of 10 November 2026 there is an unresolved question about whether Georgia's junior-doctor registration satisfies Regulation 4(b). Read the [India section](#india) and ask for written explanations.

### Is a Georgian MD accepted in the Gulf?

Each Gulf regulator sets its own requirements for recognised degrees, internship, credential verification and licensing exams. Confirm directly with the regulator in the country where you plan to work.

### How much does MBBS in Georgia cost?

Tuition is roughly **US$5,000-8,000 per year**. Including hostel, food and one-time costs, plan for about **US$50,000-65,000** over six years.

### Is Georgia part of the EU?

No. Georgia has had EU candidate status since December 2023, but the accession process has been described by EU institutions as de facto halted.

### Is IELTS or TOEFL required?

Many private universities do not require it for the international English-medium track, but requirements vary. Confirm with each university.

### Can I practise medicine in Georgia after graduating?

You can register as a junior doctor. Independent practice requires postgraduate training and, according to the Indian embassy covering Georgia, Georgian-language knowledge.

### When should I apply for the 2027 intake?

The main intake is September-October. Start your home-country eligibility steps several months ahead, since some (such as Nepal's entrance exam and NOC) have their own deadlines.
