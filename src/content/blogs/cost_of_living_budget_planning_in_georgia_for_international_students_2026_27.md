# Cost of Living & Budget Planning in Georgia for International Students (2026/27): Rent, Food, Transport & Packages

**TL;DR:** Most international students in Georgia spend roughly **$300 to $500 USD per month** (or around **1,200 to 1,800+ GEL/month** depending on city, housing setup, and winter heating) on independent living expenses—excluding tuition. **Tbilisi** is the most expensive student hub, **Batumi** sits in the middle, and **Kutaisi** typically offers lower living costs. Students who prefer predictable, hands-off budgeting can also compare independent living against an **all-inclusive living package of $9,000 USD per year**, which covers **3 meals a day, accommodation (with cleaning twice a week), Wi-Fi, and utility bills**.

## Monthly Living Cost Snapshot in Georgia (2026/27)

Living in Georgia is generally more affordable than in Western Europe or North America, though actual expenses depend on whether you share a flat or rent solo, cook at home or eat out, and live in Tbilisi versus a smaller city:

| Expense Category | Typical Monthly Estimate | Practical Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Accommodation (Rent)** | ~$80–$150 USD (shared) / ~$200–$400+ USD (private/1-BR) | Shared student apartments and dorms cost far less per person than solo city-center apartments. |
| **Food & Groceries** | ~$100–$200 USD | Cooking at home with supermarket/local market groceries keeps food costs controlled. |
| **Utilities, Gas & Internet** | ~$50–$80 USD (higher in winter) | Electricity, water, internet, and winter heating/gas vary with apartment size and season. |
| **Local Transport & Mobile** | ~$15–$35 USD | Public buses/metro and mobile data plans are inexpensive; ride-hailing adds extra cost. |
| **Health Insurance & Personal/Leisure** | ~$50–$150+ USD | Budget for required health coverage, toiletries, clothing, study materials, and personal outings. |

## City-by-City Comparison: Tbilisi, Batumi & Kutaisi

Where you study shapes both your housing options and everyday spending:

* **Tbilisi:** As Georgia's capital and largest student hub, Tbilisi offers the widest choice of universities, part-time/student jobs, and international grocery/food options, alongside higher central rent and winter heating bills.
* **Batumi:** A Black Sea coastal city with a seasonal tourism rhythm; housing and utilities often sit slightly below peak Tbilisi prices, though summer demand and seasonal shifts can affect rentals.
* **Kutaisi:** Generally lower in rent and everyday living costs than Tbilisi, making it attractive for budget-focused students.

## Independent Budget vs. All-Inclusive Living Package ($9,000/Year)

Students typically choose between managing individual monthly bills and opting for a bundled package:

* **Self-Managed / Shared Living:** Best for students comfortable signing leases, splitting utility/heating bills with roommates, shopping for groceries, and tracking monthly GEL spending—often totaling roughly **$3,600 to $6,000+ USD/year** in living costs depending on lifestyle and winter heating.
* **All-Inclusive Living Package ($9,000 USD/Year):** Provides **3 meals a day, accommodation (with cleaning twice a week), Wi-Fi, and utility bills** under one predictable annual figure—ideal for families who want meals, housing, cleaning, internet, and utilities bundled without unexpected monthly bill fluctuations.

## Smart Money-Saving Tips for Students in Georgia

* **Split Housing & Winter Heating Costs:** Sharing a flat with roommates and checking apartment insulation/heating setup before winter prevents painful gas and electricity spikes.
* **Cook at Home & Shop Local:** Buying staples, produce, and ingredients for home cooking is significantly cheaper than daily restaurant meals or delivery.
* **Use Public Transit & Plan Year-1 Setup:** Rely on public transit where convenient, and budget extra in Year 1 for initial setup, translations/recognition, insurance, and residence permit fees.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### How much money do I need per month to live in Georgia as a student?
Most students budget around **$300 to $500 USD per month** for independent living (rent, food, utilities, transport, and basic personal costs), varying by city, housing type, and winter heating.

### What is included in the $9,000 USD/year all-inclusive living option?
It covers **3 meals a day, accommodation (with cleaning twice a week), Wi-Fi, and utility bills** for the year.

### Are utilities higher during winter in Georgia?
Yes—heating, gas, and electricity bills are typically noticeably higher during colder winter months than in spring or summer.

### Should I budget extra for my first year in Georgia?
Yes—your first year usually involves upfront housing/setup costs, insurance, and immigration/recognition/residence permit fees alongside regular living expenses.
