# Welder Jobs in Slovenia: Why Romanian TIG/MIG Welders Are Making the Move (And You Can Too)

## Introduction

If you're a skilled TIG or MIG welder in Romania weighing your options, here's the good news: **you don't need a visa, a work permit, or anyone's permission to work in Slovenia.** As an EU citizen, you already have the legal right to live and work anywhere in the bloc — no sponsorship, no bureaucratic gatekeeping, just paperwork you handle yourself in a matter of weeks.

Slovenia has become a magnet for Romanian welders chasing better pay, safer job sites, and a shorter trip home than Western Europe offers. This guide walks through what the job market actually looks like, what you'll earn, what certifications you need, and exactly how to get set up — step by step.

## Why Slovenia?

Slovenia sits at the crossroads of Central and Southern Europe, bordering Italy, Austria, Hungary, and Croatia. For a Romanian worker, that geography matters:

- **Short trip home.** Slovenia is roughly a 12–14 hour drive from western Romania, or a short flight via Ljubljana, Vienna, or Zagreb — far more manageable than relocating to Germany or the Netherlands.
- **Strong industrial base.** Slovenia's metalworking, automotive components, and manufacturing sectors are well-developed and consistently short on skilled welders.
- **Lower cost of living than Western Europe**, paired with wages that are still significantly higher than Romania's.
- **Solid social security system.** Slovenia's public healthcare (ZZZS) and pension contributions are mandatory and well-run, giving you real coverage, not just a paycheck.
- **EU freedom of movement.** No visa applications, no labor market tests, no waiting on an employer to sponsor a permit — you register your residence and you're working legally.

## Job Deep Dive: TIG/MIG Welder

**Typical net salary:** €1,400–€1,900/month net, depending on experience, certification level, and industry (automotive suppliers and machinery manufacturing tend to pay at the higher end).

**Hourly equivalent:** Roughly €8–€11/hour net for standard shifts, with overtime paid at a premium — often 1.3x to 1.5x base rate.

**Certifications typically required:**
- TIG (141) and/or MIG/MAG (135) welding certificates, ideally per EN ISO 9606-1
- Prior industrial welding experience (2+ years preferred by most employers)
- Basic understanding of technical drawings/blueprints
- A forklift license is a bonus but not mandatory for welding-specific roles

**Working hours and structure:**
- Standard workweek: 40 hours, typically 8-hour shifts, Monday–Friday
- Many facilities run two- or three-shift rotations (morning/afternoon/night), with night and weekend shifts paying differentials
- Overtime is common and generally well-compensated — some employers offer 200+ hours/year of overtime availability during peak production periods

## Practical Relocation Steps

### Step 1: Register Your Residence and Get Your Tax Number
Once you arrive, you'll need to register your address at the local administrative unit (**upravna enota**) and apply for your **EMŠO** (personal identification number) and **davčna številka** (tax number). As an EU citizen, this is a registration process, not an application for permission — it typically takes 1–3 weeks.

### Step 2: Set Up Health Insurance and a Bank Account
Register with **ZZZS** (Slovenia's national health insurance institute) — your employer usually initiates this once you start work. Open a local bank account (most Slovenian banks process this quickly with your EMŠO and proof of address) so your salary can be paid without conversion fees or delays.

### Step 3: Sort Out Accommodation
You'll generally have two paths:
- **Employer-provided housing** — many industrial employers offer subsidized or free housing near the worksite, which significantly reduces your relocation costs and hassle.
- **Independent renting** — a private room or shared apartment outside major cities runs €250–€400/month, though this varies by region.
