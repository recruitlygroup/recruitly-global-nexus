## Executive Summary

Germany's care sector is under sustained pressure. Care homes, outpatient care services and hospital wards all depend on assistants and trainees who provide the day-to-day support that keeps care running. As the population ages, that demand is growing.

Many employers assume they can simply hire care assistants from abroad. **In Germany, the rules are more specific.** Nursing is a regulated field, and the legal routes for non-EU care staff depend on the candidate's qualification, language level and the type of role. Employers who understand the routes can plan realistic pipelines. Employers who do not often face rejected visas and wasted months.

## The Problem: Care Cannot Wait

### What staffing gaps mean for care providers

- **Pressure on residents and patients:** Thin teams limit time for personal care and attention.
- **Overtime and burnout:** Existing staff cover absences, which increases sickness and resignations.
- **Admission limits:** Facilities may be unable to accept new residents when rosters are short.
- **Rising agency costs:** Temporary cover is expensive and does not build a stable team.

### Why hiring abroad is not straightforward

German rules distinguish between **skilled nurses (Pflegefachpersonen)**, **nursing assistants (Pflegehilfskräfte)** and **trainees**. Each group has different qualification, language and visa requirements, and candidates with no formal care qualification cannot usually enter directly as care assistants.

## The Legal Routes for Care Staff from Nepal

According to the German government's *Make it in Germany* portal, there are several routes for nursing and care professionals from third countries. The right route depends on the candidate.

### Route 1: Paid vocational training (Pflege-Ausbildung)

Candidates can enter Germany for the **three-year generalist nursing training (Pflegefachfrau/Pflegefachmann)** on a vocational training visa. Trainees earn a salary during training and, after qualifying, can work as fully qualified nurses.

- **Best for:** Candidates with the required school-leaving qualification and strong motivation, who can reach the required German level.
- **Language:** Requirements depend on the training provider and federal state. Many providers expect B1 or B2 before the start of training.
- **Employer benefit:** A trainee who qualifies with your organization is a long-term investment, and loyalty is typically high.

### Route 2: Recognized foreign qualification for assistant roles

Candidates who hold a foreign nursing or care qualification that has been **recognized in Germany for assisting in nursing** can work as nursing assistants. This applies to activities based on state-recognized vocational training, such as nursing assistant or geriatric care assistant roles. Applicants submit their documents through the recognition process, handled by the competent state authority.

- **Best for:** Nepali candidates with formal vocational nursing or care training that the authority assesses as equivalent for assistant-level work.
- **Employer benefit:** Faster entry to the workforce than full training, with a clear legal basis.

### Route 3: Recognition partnership and partial recognition

For **skilled nurses** whose qualifications are partly but not fully recognized, there are routes to enter Germany to complete compensation measures, including the **Recognition Partnership** (Anerkennungspartnerschaft). Sources describe a requirement of at least **A2 German** for this route, approval from the Federal Employment Agency, and a prompt start of the adaptation measures after arrival. The full professional licence requires **B2**.

- **Best for:** Qualified nurses who are close to full recognition.
- **Employer benefit:** A skilled colleague who becomes a fully licensed nurse once the measures are completed.

### Route 4: Fully qualified nurses

Nurses whose qualifications are fully recognized can apply for a work visa for qualified professionals. For BSc-qualified nurses with B2 German, see our guide: [Germany Nurse Shortage 2026: Hiring BSc Nurses from Nepal](/blog/germany-nurse-shortage-recruit-bsc-nurses-nepal-b2-german).

**Important:** Rules, language thresholds and procedures vary by federal state and change over time. Employers should confirm requirements with the competent authority in their state and use the official portals, *Anerkennung in Deutschland* and *Make it in Germany*.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can care assistants from Nepal come to Germany without a qualification?

Not usually as assistants. Assistant roles generally require either completed state-recognized training in Germany or a foreign qualification recognized for assisting in nursing. Candidates without these can consider the paid vocational training route.

### Which language level is needed?

It depends on the pathway and federal state. Some routes require A2 for the visa stage, many training providers expect B1 or B2, and the full nursing licence requires B2.

### Can candidates train as nurses while earning a salary?

Yes. The three-year Pflege-Ausbildung is a paid vocational training program open to international applicants on a vocational training visa.
