# From Student in Finland to Worker in Slovenia: Warehouse & Hospitality Jobs for Non-EU Students

## Introduction

If you're a non-EU student currently holding a temporary residence card (TRC) in Finland, you've probably already run the numbers: student life in Finland is expensive, part-time work hours are limited, and the path to a stable, full-time job after graduation isn't always straightforward. Slovenia offers a genuinely different option — real, full-time warehouse and hospitality vacancies specifically open to students and recent graduates, with a legal work permit process that's faster and simpler than most people expect.

This guide walks through what those jobs actually pay, what the permit process really involves, realistic timelines, and — importantly — an honest look at when and how you can eventually bring family members to join you, since that process works differently than many people assume.

---

## Why Slovenia for Students Moving From Finland?

Your Finnish student residence permit is valid for Finland only — it doesn't carry over or accelerate your paperwork in Slovenia, since Slovenia has its own separate work and residence permit system for non-EU nationals (the **single permit**). That said, Slovenia is an appealing next step for several reasons:

- **Active demand for student and early-career workers** in warehouse/logistics and hospitality — sectors that consistently need shift-based staff and are open to candidates without years of local experience.
- **A structured, employer-sponsored process.** Once a Slovenian employer offers you a role, they initiate your single permit application — it's a regulated pathway, not an informal arrangement.
- **Central European location**, with reasonable travel connections back to your home region and across the rest of the EU.
- **Lower cost of living than Finland** in most day-to-day categories, particularly rent outside Ljubljana.

## Job Deep Dive: Warehouse & Hospitality Roles

### Warehouse / Logistics Operative
**Typical net salary:** €900–€1,300/month net, with overtime common during peak logistics periods.

**What's typically expected:**
- No prior EU work experience required for entry-level roles — most employers train on-site
- A forklift license is a strong plus and often boosts pay; some employers will sponsor this training after your probation period
- Ability to work rotating shifts (morning/afternoon/night)

### Hospitality Roles (Hotels, Restaurants, Resorts)
**Typical net salary:** €900–€1,300/month net, sometimes higher with tips/service charges in tourist-heavy roles.

**Common positions:** housekeeping, kitchen assistant, waitstaff, front-of-house support.

**What's typically expected:**
- Willingness to work weekends and holidays — standard in hospitality
- Basic English is usually sufficient at entry level
- Prior hospitality or service experience is a plus but not always mandatory

**Working hours for both sectors:** Standard 40-hour week across 8-hour shifts is typical, with clearly defined overtime pay for extra hours.

## Practical Relocation Steps

### Step 1: Secure a Confirmed Job Offer
This is the starting point — a Slovenian employer must offer you a role and initiate your **single permit** (combined work and residence permit) application through Slovenia's Employment Service. Your Finnish student status doesn't transfer or expedite this; the process starts fresh.

### Step 2: Application and Processing
Once your employer submits the application, official Slovenian sources indicate single permit processing generally runs **around 60 to 90 days** for a complete application, though straightforward cases with all documents in order can move somewhat faster. Build your timeline around 2–3 months rather than assuming the fastest possible outcome.

### Step 3: Visa and Travel
If you require an entry visa, you'll apply for it once your single permit is approved, typically at the Slovenian embassy or consulate covering your situation. Since you're currently in Finland, confirm with the relevant Slovenian diplomatic mission whether you can apply based on your Finnish residence rather than needing to return to your home country first — this varies by case.

### Step 4: Register in Slovenia
On arrival, register your address at the local **upravna enota**, obtain your **EMŠO** and **davčna številka** (tax number), register for health insurance through **ZZZS**, and open a Slovenian bank account. Your employer typically supports the ZZZS registration as part of onboarding.

### Step 5: Accommodation
Many warehouse and hospitality employers hiring students offer staff or shared accommodation as part of the package — confirm this in writing, including cost (if any) and what happens if your employment ends.

## An Honest Note on Bringing Family Members

This is worth being direct about, because it affects real planning decisions: **Slovenian law generally requires a third-country national worker to hold a temporary residence permit for two continuous years, with at least one year of validity remaining, before becoming eligible to sponsor family reunification.** This applies to standard single-permit holders, including warehouse and hospitality workers — it is not something that happens within the same timeframe as your initial work permit.

There are narrow exceptions (for example, EU Blue Card holders for highly skilled roles have a different, faster family reunification pathway under EU rules), but they don't typically apply to entry-level warehouse or hospitality positions. If bringing family members is a major part of your decision to relocate, plan around the two-year timeline rather than expecting it sooner, and confirm your specific eligibility with the administrative unit handling your permit once you're settled in Slovenia.
