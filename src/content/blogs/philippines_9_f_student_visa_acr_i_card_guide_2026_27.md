# Philippines 9(f) Student Visa & ACR I-Card Guide (2026/27): Requirements, Embassy vs. Conversion Process, Fees & Document Attestation

**TL;DR:** International students aged **18 and older** enrolling in a full degree program (Bachelor's, Master's, MD, or PhD) in the Philippines must hold a **9(f) Student Visa** and an **ACR I-Card (Alien Certificate of Registration Identity Card)**. Students under 18 or enrolled in non-degree courses apply for a **Special Study Permit (SSP)** instead. Depending on your nationality and university instructions, you either apply for the 9(f) visa at a Philippine Embassy before departure or enter on a **9(a) Temporary Visitor Visa** and convert to a 9(f) status through your university’s accredited Bureau of Immigration (BI) liaison office. Published BI conversion fees are **₱9,720** plus **$50 USD** for the ACR I-Card, and the visa is renewed annually alongside mandatory BI Annual Reporting.

## 9(f) Student Visa vs. Special Study Permit (SSP): Which One Do You Need?

The Philippine Bureau of Immigration (BI) separates foreign learners into two distinct legal categories based on **age** and **program type**:

| Permit / Visa Type | Who Qualifies | Eligible Programs | Validity & Renewal |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **9(f) Student Visa** | Foreign nationals aged **18 years or older** admitted to a BI- and CHED-accredited higher education institution. | Full degree programs above high school (BS Nursing, BS-MD / MD, Engineering, IT, MBA, PhD). | Issued for **1 year** initially; renewed annually throughout your degree. |
| **Special Study Permit (SSP)** | Foreign students **under 18 years old** OR learners taking short non-degree courses. | Short courses, ESL/language programs, pilot training, exchange terms, or first semester before turning 18. | Valid for **6 months** (paired with an active 9(a) tourist visa extension). |

> **Critical Accreditation Rule:** A 9(f) application is **never filed by the student alone**—it is a joint filing between you and your admitting university. Only schools holding active **Bureau of Immigration accreditation to accept foreign students** can issue a valid Notice of Acceptance (NOA). Always verify your university’s BI accreditation before paying tuition deposits.

## Two Ways to Get Your 9(f) Student Visa: Embassy Filing vs. In-Country Conversion

Universities in Manila, Cebu, and Davao process international arrivals using one of two official pathways. Always follow your admitting university's written instruction:

### Route A: Apply Abroad at a Philippine Embassy or Consulate (Pre-Departure)
* **How it works:** After issuing your Notice of Acceptance, the university forwards your endorsed file to the Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA) in Manila, which authorizes the Philippine Embassy/Consulate in your home country (e.g., New Delhi for Nepal/India, Abu Dhabi/Riyadh for GCC residents, or Abuja/Nairobi) to issue your 9(f) visa sticker before you fly.
* **After landing:** You must report to your school and register with the Bureau of Immigration within **7 days of arrival** to capture biometrics and claim your ACR I-Card.

### Route B: Enter on a 9(a) Visitor Visa & Convert On-Campus (Most Common)
* **How it works:** You enter the Philippines either visa-free (for eligible nationalities) or on a **9(a) Temporary Visitor Visa** obtained at a Philippine Embassy. Once you arrive on campus, complete medical screening, and register, your university's International Student Office submits your **Conversion to 9(f) Student Visa** petition directly to the Bureau of Immigration.
* **Key Timing Rule:** Under BI rules, your tourist visa must have **at least 21 days of valid authorized stay remaining** at the time of conversion filing, and the conversion petition must be lodged within **15 days** of your official Certificate of Acceptance/Admission.

## Document Authentication: Apostille vs. Embassy Legalization

Before any home-country document is accepted by a Philippine university or the Bureau of Immigration, it must be legally authenticated:

1. **Students from Hague Apostille Convention Countries** *(e.g., India, Indonesia, Nigeria, Kenya, South Africa, Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Oman, EU/UK)*:
   * Obtain an **Apostille stamp** from your country's designated competent authority (such as the Ministry of External Affairs / Foreign Affairs) on your academic transcripts, diploma, birth certificate, and national police clearance. No separate Philippine Embassy legalization is required for Apostilled documents.
2. **Students from Non-Apostille Countries** *(e.g., Nepal, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait)*:
   * First attest your original documents at your home country's **Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MOFA)**.
   * Second, submit the MOFA-attested documents to the **Philippine Embassy or Consulate** with jurisdiction over your country for consular authentication (often still referred to by schools as "Red Ribbon" authentication).

## Complete 9(f) Student Visa & Conversion Document Checklist (2026/27)

Prepare **two complete sets** (original + clear photocopies) along with English translations for any document not issued in English:

### Student-Provided Documents
* **Valid Passport:** Minimum 12 months remaining validity, plus copies of the bio-page, latest Philippine arrival stamp, valid authorized stay stamp, and Bureau of Quarantine (BOQ) stamp (for in-country conversion).
* **Consolidated General Application Form (CGAF):** Completed in two original copies (one for the 9(f) visa, one for the ACR I-Card).
* **Authenticated Academic Records:** High school / university transcripts and graduation certificates.
* **Authenticated Police Clearance Certificate:** Issued by your home country’s national police force. *(Note: If you have already stayed in the Philippines for 6 months or more prior to filing, you must also obtain a local **NBI Clearance**).*
* **Proof of Financial Capacity:** Recent bank statements, bank certificate, scholarship letter, or notarized Affidavit of Support showing sufficient funds for tuition and living expenses (typically **$5,000–$8,000 USD** equivalent).
* **Medical & Quarantine Clearance:** Medical certificate of fitness to study from a BI-accredited hospital (including chest X-ray, blood tests, and drug screening) stamped by the **Bureau of Quarantine (BOQ)**.
* **2x2 Inch Passport Photos:** White background, taken within the last 3 months.

### University-Provided Documents
* **Notice of Acceptance (NOA):** Bearing the university’s official dry seal.
* **Joint Letter Request & Registrar Endorsement:** Addressed to the BI Commissioner and signed by the university's authorized BI liaison officer.
* **CHED Certificate of Eligibility for Admission (CEA):** Mandatory specifically for **Doctor of Medicine (MD)** and **Doctor of Dental Medicine (DMD)** applicants.
* **BI Clearance Certificate & School Accreditation ID Copy**.

## What Is the ACR I-Card & Annual Student Compliance Rules

Once your 9(f) visa is approved, you complete fingerprint and photo biometrics at the Bureau of Immigration to receive your **ACR I-Card**—a microchip-embedded identity card that serves as your official government ID in the Philippines for opening a local bank account, signing apartment leases, and domestic travel.

**Four rules every 9(f) student visa holder must follow to stay in legal status:**
1. **No Off-Campus Employment:** A 9(f) student visa strictly prohibits paid employment in the Philippines. Violating work restrictions leads to visa cancellation and deportation.
2. **Maintain Full-Time Academic Load:** Failing to enroll or dropping below full-time status without an approved Leave of Absence (LOA) triggers automatic visa cancellation by your university.
3. **Complete the January–February Annual Report:** Between January 1 and March 1 each year, every ACR I-Card holder must file their **Annual Report** with the Bureau of Immigration (in person or via the BI e-Services portal).
4. **Secure an ECC Before Flying Home for Holidays:** If you have lived in the Philippines for **6 months or longer** and travel home for semester breaks, you must obtain an **Emigration Clearance Certificate (ECC)** and Special Return Certificate (SRC) prior to departure so your 9(f) visa remains valid upon re-entry.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### How long does 9(f) student visa processing take in the Philippines?
Embassy pre-departure processing typically takes **3 to 6 weeks** depending on DFA Manila endorsement timelines. In-country conversion from a 9(a) visitor visa to a 9(f) student visa generally takes **4 to 8 weeks** from the date your university submits a complete file to the Bureau of Immigration.

### Can I enter the Philippines on a tourist visa and convert to a 9(f) student visa?
Yes. In-country conversion from a 9(a) Temporary Visitor Visa (or visa-free entry) to a 9(f) student visa is the most common pathway used by Philippine universities. Make sure your tourist stay has at least **21 days remaining** when your school lodges the conversion application.

### Do I need an IELTS or TOEFL score for a Philippine 9(f) student visa?
The Bureau of Immigration does not enforce a blanket IELTS score for the 9(f) visa. However, individual universities may request proof of English-medium secondary schooling or an internal English placement assessment if you are from a non-English-speaking curriculum.

### Can I transfer universities in the Philippines while holding a 9(f) student visa?
Yes, but you cannot simply enroll at a new university. You must secure an **Honorable Dismissal / Transfer Credential** from your current school, obtain a new Notice of Acceptance, and file a **CHED and Bureau of Immigration transfer endorsement** before your current 9(f) visa expires.

### What happens if I graduate or leave the Philippines permanently?
Before departing permanently, your university’s International Student Office will guide you through **downgrading your 9(f) student visa back to a 9(a) temporary visitor status** and securing an **Emigration Clearance Certificate (ECC)** at the Bureau of Immigration.
