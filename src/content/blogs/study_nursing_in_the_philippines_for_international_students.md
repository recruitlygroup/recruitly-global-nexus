# Study Nursing in the Philippines for International Students: 2026/27 Tuition, Top Universities, 9(f) Visa & Global Licensing (NCLEX / UK / GCC)

**TL;DR:** International students can study nursing in the Philippines through a standard **4-year Bachelor of Science in Nursing (BSN)** or a specialized **International Nursing Program (INP) / second-degree bridging track** at accredited institutions such as Arellano University, University of the Philippines (UP) Manila, Southwestern University (SWU) PHINMA, and University of Santo Tomas (UST). Annual nursing tuition ranges from **₱40,000 to ₱250,000+ ($700–$4,500 USD)**, with monthly living expenses averaging **₱15,000 to ₱40,000 ($260–$700 USD)**. Applicants must secure a **9(f) Student Visa** (conversion fee: ₱9,720 + $50 ACR I-Card) and plan ahead for foreign nursing licensure, as Philippine Nurse Licensure Examination (PNLE) eligibility is restricted under Republic Act No. 9173 unless home-country reciprocity applies.

---

## Why the Philippines Is a Global Launchpad for International Nursing Students

The Philippines is widely recognized as the world's leading trainer of internationally mobile nurses, supplying healthcare systems across the **United States, the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, Germany, and the GCC**. Unlike many destinations where international students face steep tuition or a mandatory local language barrier before entering hospital wards, Philippine nursing education is engineered from day one for global practice:

* **100% English-Medium Instruction & Clinical Practice:** Lectures, medical charts, patient communication, and faculty evaluations are conducted in English—eliminating language delays during hospital rotations.
* **US-Modeled Curriculum:** Philippine BSN programs are closely aligned with American nursing education standards, giving graduates a direct academic foundation for the **NCLEX-RN** (USA/Canada/Australia) and **NMC OSCE** (UK).
* **High-Volume Clinical Exposure:** Under the Commission on Higher Education (CHED) curriculum, students complete extensive **Related Learning Experience (RLE)** hours across tertiary hospitals, maternity wards, operating theaters, and community clinics—producing the documented clinical hours required by foreign nursing boards.
* **High Return on Investment (ROI):** Total four-year tuition and living costs in Manila or Cebu often cost less than a single academic year of nursing school in North America, the UK, or Australia.

## BS Nursing vs. International Nursing & Bridging Programs

International applicants—particularly students from **Nepal, India, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, Nigeria, Kenya, and the GCC**—generally choose between three structured pathways depending on their prior academic background:

| Pathway | Typical Duration | Who It Is For | Primary Outcome |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Standard BS Nursing (BSN)** | 4 Years (8 Semesters) | High school (10+2 / A-Level) graduates with Science subjects (Biology, Chemistry, Physics). | Full BSN degree + documented RLE clinical hours for foreign RN exams. |
| **International Nursing Program (INP)** | 3.5 – 4 Years (or customized) | Foreign students seeking an international cohort with dedicated NCLEX/CGFNS prep. | BSN tailored for US/international licensure without local board prerequisites. |
| **Second-Course / Bridging BSN** | 1.5 – 2.5 Years | Graduates who already hold a prior bachelor's degree in health sciences or allied fields. | Accelerated BSN completion via credit transfer (subject to CHED evaluation). |

> **Important Regulatory Note on Licensure:** Under the **Philippine Nursing Act of 2002 (Republic Act No. 9173)**, sitting for the local Philippine Nurse Licensure Examination (PNLE) requires Filipino citizenship or formal **foreign reciprocity** (proof that your home country grants Filipino nurses the exact same right to take its national licensing exam). Most international students enroll specifically to earn their **BSN degree and clinical transcript** to sit directly for foreign licensing exams (like the **NCLEX-RN** or their home country’s nursing council exam) rather than practicing locally in the Philippines.

## Admission Requirements for Foreign Nursing Applicants

To qualify for a BSN program in the Philippines, applicants must prepare authenticated academic and civil documents:

1. **Academic Transcripts & Certificates:** High school (Grade 12 / 10+2 / A-Level) mark sheets showing passes in **Biology, Chemistry, and English** (typically minimum 50%–60% aggregate depending on the university).
2. **Document Authentication (Apostille / Red Ribbon):** Because the Philippines is a member of the **Hague Apostille Convention**, students from Apostille-member countries must have their academic transcripts and police clearance apostilled. Students from non-Apostille countries (such as Nepal) must complete **Ministry of Foreign Affairs attestation followed by Philippine Embassy/Consulate legalization**.
3. **Valid Passport:** Minimum 6 to 12 months validity beyond your intended arrival date.
4. **Proof of Financial Capacity:** Bank statements or sponsor affidavits showing sufficient funds to cover first-year tuition and living expenses (typically **$5,000–$7,000 USD** or ₱300,000–₱400,000 equivalent).
5. **Police Clearance Certificate:** Issued by your home country’s national police authority.
6. **Medical & Immunization Clearance:** Chest X-ray, Hepatitis B screening/vaccination, MMR, Tdap, and quarantine medical clearance—strictly required before hospital clinical duty.
7. **English Proficiency:** Usually waived if your prior secondary schooling was conducted in English; otherwise, IELTS (5.5–6.0) or an internal university English placement test applies.

## Step-by-Step: 9(f) Student Visa & Enrollment Process

Programs longer than six months require a **9(f) Student Visa** issued through the Philippine Bureau of Immigration (BI). Depending on your nationality and university guidance, you either apply for the 9(f) visa at a Philippine Embassy/Consulate before flying or enter on a 9(a) tourist visa and convert to a 9(f) visa on campus through your university's international liaison office.

1. **Submit Online Application & Academic Records:** Send scanned transcripts and passport copies to your shortlisted university's International Student Office.
2. **Complete Entrance Assessment / Interview:** Take the university's nursing entrance exam or online admissions interview.
3. **Receive Your Notice of Acceptance (NOA):** Once admitted and initial fees are settled, the university issues an official **NOA** and **Certificate of Eligibility for Admission (CEA)**.
4. **Apply for / Convert to the 9(f) Student Visa:** Submit your NOA, authenticated documents, medical exam (including BI-accredited quarantine clearance), and visa fees.
   * **BI Visa Conversion Fee:** Approx. **₱9,720**
   * **ACR I-Card (Alien Certificate of Registration):** **$50 USD**
   * **Annual Visa Extension:** Approx. **₱4,020 + $50 USD** per year
5. **Complete Hospital Immunization & RLE Onboarding:** Finish mandatory hospital vaccinations and order clinical uniforms before your first ward rotation.

## Clinical Training (RLE) & Life as an International Nursing Student

From your second year onward, your schedule splits between classroom lectures and **Related Learning Experience (RLE)** hospital shifts. You will rotate through medical-surgical wards, pediatrics, obstetrics, psychiatric nursing, and rural/community health units under direct clinical instructor supervision.

**Three practical tips to succeed in Philippine nursing school:**
* **Archive Every Clinical Case & RLE Record:** Keep certified copies of your **Summary of Related Learning Experience (RLE)** and clinical case logs. Foreign credential evaluators (such as CGFNS for the US or nursing councils in Nepal, India, and the UK) verify exact theory-to-clinical hour ratios.
* **Live Within Walking Distance of Your Base Hospital:** Early-morning hospital shifts frequently start at **6:00 AM or 7:00 AM**. Living in a dorm near your university or assigned teaching hospital saves hours of Metro Manila or Cebu traffic.
* **Never Miss Annual BI Reporting:** Every January–February, all ACR I-Card holders must complete the Bureau of Immigration **Annual Report** (in person or via the BI e-Services portal) alongside their annual 9(f) visa extension.

## Global Licensing After Graduation: How It Works

Earning your BSN in the Philippines sets up multiple international career pathways:

* **United States (NCLEX-RN):** Because Philippine BSN programs mirror US nursing standards, graduates can apply directly through a US State Board of Nursing (such as New York, Texas, or Illinois—which do not require a local Philippine license for foreign-educated nurses), complete **CGFNS / CES credential verification**, and sit for the **NCLEX-RN**.
* **United Kingdom (NMC):** Graduates pass the **IELTS/OET**, complete the **NMC Computer-Based Test (CBT)**, and travel to the UK to pass the practical **OSCE** exam.
* **Middle East / GCC (DHA, HAAD/DOH, MOH, Prometric):** Employers in the UAE, Saudi Arabia, and Qatar actively recruit Philippine-trained BSN graduates via Prometric exams and DataFlow primary-source verification.
* **Returning to Your Home Country (e.g., Nepal / India):** Students returning home must verify prior accreditation before enrolling—such as **Nepal Nursing Council (NNC)** eligibility rules for Nepali students or **Indian Nursing Council (INC)** equivalence guidelines for Indian applicants.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Can foreign students study nursing in the Philippines?
Yes. International students can enroll in a four-year BS Nursing program or a dedicated International Nursing Program (INP) at CHED- and Bureau of Immigration-accredited universities such as Arellano University, UP Manila, SWU PHINMA, and UST.

### How long is BS Nursing in the Philippines for international students?
A standard Bachelor of Science in Nursing takes **4 years (8 semesters)** to complete, combining classroom coursework with supervised hospital and community clinical rotations (RLE).

### What is an International Nursing Program (INP) in the Philippines?
An International Nursing Program (such as the track offered at Arellano University) is a specialized BSN curriculum designed specifically for foreign students. It aligns clinical training and exam preparation directly toward international licensing exams like the NCLEX-RN rather than local Philippine board licensure.

### How much does it cost to study nursing in the Philippines as a foreigner?
Annual tuition for BS Nursing generally ranges from **₱40,000 to ₱250,000+ ($700–$4,500 USD)** per year, plus ₱8,000–₱25,000 in annual clinical/RLE fees. Living expenses in Manila or Cebu average **₱15,000 to ₱40,000 ($260–$700 USD)** per month.

### Do international nursing students need a visa?
Yes. Any foreign student enrolling in a degree program longer than six months must hold a **9(f) Student Visa** and an **ACR I-Card** issued by the Philippine Bureau of Immigration, which is renewed annually throughout your degree.

### Do I need to pass the Philippine NLE if I only want to work overseas?
No. Under Republic Act No. 9173, foreign nationals generally cannot sit for the Philippine Nurse Licensure Examination (PNLE) unless their home country has a formal reciprocity treaty with the Philippines. Instead, international graduates use their Philippine BSN degree, official transcript, and RLE clinical hour records to apply directly for licensing exams in their target country (such as the US NCLEX-RN, UK NMC, or home-country nursing council exams).
