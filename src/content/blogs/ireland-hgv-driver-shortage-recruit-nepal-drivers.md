## The Problem: Every Idle Truck Costs You Money

### What driver gaps do to a fleet

- **Idle vehicles:** Trucks that could be earning sit unused when drivers are unavailable.
- **Missed deliveries and lost contracts:** Reliability is the product in logistics.
- **Overtime and driver fatigue:** Remaining drivers cover shifts, raising costs and risk and driving further resignations.
- **Rate pressure:** Competition for a limited pool pushes wages up and margins down.

### Why the shortage persists

- **Ageing driver workforce:** Retirements outpace new entrants.
- **Barriers to entry:** Licensing, medical requirements and Driver CPC training take time and money for new drivers.
- **A thinner EU pool:** Hiring from within the EU no longer covers demand for many employers.

Waiting for the domestic pipeline to recover means lost capacity. Planned international recruitment is the practical alternative.

## How Hiring Non-EEA HGV Drivers Works in Ireland

### The permit route

According to Irish recruitment and immigration commentary, HGV drivers are hired through the **General Employment Permit (GEP)**. HGV driving is not a Critical Skills occupation, and it is not on the Ineligible Occupations List, so drivers are eligible for a GEP. Irish policy has also exempted non-EU HGV drivers from the GEP quota since 2021, as reported by immigration advisers.

Commonly cited GEP requirements for HGV roles include:

- **A minimum salary threshold** (reported as **€36,605** in 2026)
- **A Labour Market Needs Test**, typically a 28-day advertisement period
- **The 50/50 rule**, meaning at least half of an employer's workforce must be EEA nationals

**Important:** Thresholds, lists and rules change. Irish authorities updated the occupation lists in May 2026, and employers should check the current requirements with the Department of Enterprise, Tourism and Employment (DETE) before applying. Only the employer can apply for a GEP.

### Licensing: the step that shapes the timeline

An employment permit does not allow a driver to drive an HGV on Irish roads. Drivers must also hold a valid Irish licence in the correct category.

- **Licence recognition:** Licences from EU/EEA countries are recognized. Licences from a limited number of other countries can be exchanged. For drivers from countries without an exchange agreement, the licence is not accepted and the driver must **pass the Irish theory and practical tests**. The Road Safety Authority (RSA) publishes the current list of exchange countries, and employers should confirm the position for each candidate.
- **Driver CPC:** The **Certificate of Professional Competence** is mandatory for professional HGV drivers in Ireland, with periodic training required.
- **Digital tachograph card:** Required for drivers operating regulated vehicles.
- **Medical and identity requirements:** Needed for the licence process.

**Planning point:** Because licensing can take time after arrival, realistic planning is essential. Employers who understand this up front can schedule onboarding, supervised driving or non-driving duties appropriately and avoid unrealistic expectations.

### Can non-EEA HGV drivers get an employment permit in Ireland?

HGV drivers are generally hired through the General Employment Permit and are not on the Ineligible Occupations List. Salary thresholds, the Labour Market Needs Test and the 50/50 rule apply, so employers should confirm current requirements with DETE.

### Can a Nepali driver drive an HGV in Ireland on a Nepali licence?

Not automatically. Licences from countries without an exchange agreement are not accepted, and drivers typically need to pass Irish theory and practical tests. Check the RSA's current list of exchange countries.

### Is Driver CPC required?

Yes. The Certificate of Professional Competence is mandatory for professional HGV drivers in Ireland.
