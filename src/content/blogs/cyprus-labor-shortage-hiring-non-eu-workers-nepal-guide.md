# Cyprus Labor Shortage 2026: The Employer's Guide to Hiring Non-EU Workers from Nepal

## Executive Summary

Cyprus has more work than workers. Hotels cannot fully staff peak season, builders are losing project months to crew shortages, and cleaning and support teams are hard to fill across almost every sector.

Employers are responding by turning to third-country (non-EU) nationals. A Flash Eurobarometer survey of SMEs found that **65 percent of Cypriot SMEs that tried to recruit non-EU workers called the process difficult**, compared with an EU average of 54 percent, with lengthy and complex procedures as the main cause. Demand is clear. The bottleneck is the hiring process.

This guide is for hotel owners, contractors, facility managers, HR directors and recruitment agencies in Cyprus. It covers where the shortages are sharpest, how third-country hiring works, why Nepali workers are a strong fit, and what a compliant end-to-end process looks like with **Recruitly Group**.

## Where Cyprus Is Short of Workers

Shortages are not evenly spread. Three areas stand out.

### Hospitality: seasonal peaks without enough staff

High seasonal demand and a limited local pool leave hotels and resorts short in **housekeeping, kitchen assistance, line cooking and F&B front-of-house**. Each unfilled role means slower room turnaround, reduced dining capacity, overtime for existing teams and pressure on guest reviews.

**Read the full guide:** [Solving Cyprus's Hospitality Labor Shortage](/blog/cyprus-hospitality-labor-shortage-nepal-staff)

### Construction: delayed projects and rising costs

Industry bodies including OSEOK and ETEK have warned that the construction shortage is delaying projects and raising costs. Industry estimates put the gap at roughly 5,000 workers, and the greatest demand is for **steel fixers, formwork carpenters, skilled construction workers, site foremen and supervisors**. The departure of many Syrian workers under the government's voluntary repatriation scheme has thinned the pool further.

**Read the full guide:** [Cyprus Construction Labor Shortage: Recruiting Steel Fixers, Carpenters and Site Workers](/blog/cyprus-construction-labor-shortage-nepal-workers)

### Cleaning and support services: the most common non-EU hire

In the same Eurobarometer survey, **cleaning and support services were the occupations for which Cypriot SMEs most often hired third-country workers, at 42 percent**, ahead of skilled construction workers at 29 percent and food preparation and processing staff at 26 percent.

## The Structural Picture

These shortages share common causes.

- **A changing workforce:** According to the Cyprus Statistical Office, as reported in business press, Cypriot nationals were 72.0 percent of those in employment in 2025, down from 80.1 percent in 2015, with much of the growth in employment coming from EU and third-country nationals.
- **Seasonal and project-driven demand:** Tourism and construction both create sharp peaks that local supply cannot absorb.
- **Employer reliance on non-EU hiring:** Employer organizations have described third-country recruitment as an essential route for private-sector growth.

Waiting for the labor market to correct itself means lost revenue, delayed projects and burned-out teams. A planned recruitment pipeline is the practical alternative.

## How Third-Country Hiring Works in Cyprus

The process is regulated, and employers remain responsible for compliance. In outline:

1. **Employer application:** The employer applies to the Department of Labour at the Ministry of Labour, which assesses whether there is a genuine shortage and may issue initial approval.
2. **Permits:** Workers need the appropriate entry, temporary residence and employment permits before starting work.
3. **Sector rules and quotas:** Employment is allowed only in sectors where non-EU hiring is permitted under labor migration policy. Industry federations have reported that firms may employ third-country nationals up to 30 percent of their workforce, rising to 50 percent where a collective agreement applies.
4. **Role accuracy:** Workers must be employed in the role and sector for which the permit was approved. Cyprus authorities have flagged misuse of cleaning permits for other work, such as on construction sites.

**Important:** Rules, quotas and procedures change. Always confirm current requirements with the Cyprus Department of Labour and the relevant migration authorities before applying.

### Where employers get stuck

In the Eurobarometer data, **47 percent of Cypriot SMEs that attempted third-country hiring named complex or lengthy procedures as the main obstacle**, against 31 percent across the EU. Cypriot SMEs ranked immigration and relocation assistance as the most useful support, at 38 percent versus 18 percent at EU level, yet only 17 percent said they were familiar with the support services available.
