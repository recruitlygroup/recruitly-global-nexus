# Hiring Bulgarian Workers in Slovenia: Direct Hire vs. Posted Workers

## Introduction

If you run a plant, construction firm, logistics operation or hotel in Slovenia, you already know the problem: open positions stay open for months, and the local candidate pool is not growing. Bulgaria is one of the fastest EU sources of skilled and semi-skilled labour. Because Bulgarians are EU citizens, **no work permit, no quota and no single-permit process is required.** That is the key difference from hiring workers from outside the EU.

This guide explains the two legal models, what compliance involves in 2026, and how a Bulgaria-registered agency shortens your time-to-hire.

## Compliance Checklist (What Protects You)

### For posted workers

Slovenia regulates posting under the **Transnational Provision of Services Act** (ZČmIS-1, amended in 2023), which implements Directive 96/71/EC.

1. **A1 certificate** for each worker, issued by Bulgaria's National Revenue Agency (NRA). It confirms that social security stays in Bulgaria and can cover a maximum of 24 months.
2. **Registration with the Slovenian Employment Service (ZRSZ)** before the service starts, done online. It covers the foreign employer, a contact person for supervisory authorities, the type of service, each posted worker and job title, the posting period and the work location.
3. **Slovenian core terms apply** under the Employment Relationships Act and the relevant collective agreement, including working time, rest, health and safety, and pay. The 2026 statutory minimum wage is **€1,481.88 gross per month** (about €1,000 net), in force since 1 January 2026.
4. **Documents ready for inspection:** the service contract, employment contracts, payslips, proof of salary payment, working-time records and a valid A1. Keep them for 24 months after the posting ends and have Slovenian translations available.
5. **Know the penalties:** the 2023 amendments set fines of **€2,000 to €60,000** for foreign employers and **€200 to €6,000** for their responsible persons.

### For direct hires

1. Written Slovenian employment contract.
2. Employer registration of the worker for social insurance before the start date.
3. Standard payroll, tax and ZZZS health insurance processes.
4. Pay at or above the €1,481.88 gross minimum, plus the annual holiday allowance (regres), which must be at least the minimum wage and paid by 1 July.
5. Occupational health and safety training, with documents in a language the worker understands.

*Rules and thresholds change. Always confirm with your Slovenian legal or tax adviser. We coordinate with them as part of onboarding.*

## FAQ

### Do I need a work permit for Bulgarian workers?

No. Bulgarian citizens have full access to the Slovenian labour market as EU citizens.

### Who pays social contributions for posted workers?

They stay in Bulgaria under the A1 certificate. Slovenian minimum pay and working conditions still apply.

### How long can a posting last?

The A1 can cover up to 24 months. Under the Slovenian rules, a service posting runs for 12 months and can be extended to 18 months with notification to the Employment Service.

### Can I switch from posting to direct hire later?

Yes. Many clients start with a project-based posting and convert strong workers to permanent contracts. [Insert your conversion terms.]
